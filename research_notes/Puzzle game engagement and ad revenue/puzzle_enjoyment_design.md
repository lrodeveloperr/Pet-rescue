## 1. Difficulty curves and pacing: sawtooth, breathers, mechanic cadence, target attempt/failure rates

### Takeaway
Successful level-based puzzle games ramp difficulty in repeating cycles. A new element arrives with 2–3 easy "learning" levels, difficulty climbs to a spike, and a breather or bonus level follows. In Royal Match's first 100 levels, normal levels average about 1.2 attempts, hard levels about 1.6 and super-hard peaks about 2.5. King's data says a hard level should also be a short one, and its fun metric is built from time-to-abandon and time-to-pass, not from pass rate alone. Experimental evidence also cautions against assuming harder is better: when players do not choose the difficulty, the easiest variants tend to engage best.

### Cited Findings
**King / Candy Crush**
- [DESIGNER/DATA, GDC talk "Sugary Statistics", most likely GDC 2024] Jan Wedekind (Head of Central Insights) and Xavier Guardiola (Director of Data Science) presented what King learned from 10+ years and more than 15,000 Candy Crush levels. They described a genre-agnostic model "based in theoretical physics" that characterizes "true" engagement per level, and they stressed "considering the whole game progression when tweaking or fixing any individual level." — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034392/Sugary-Statistics-What-We-Have)
- [DESIGNER/DATA] King scores how fun a level is by combining a "time to abandon" figure with a "time to pass" figure. Time to abandon is meant to capture intrinsic motivation and engagement. Hard levels can still be fun, and easy levels can show little churn and still not be much fun. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/) (the search tool dated this article about 915 days before Oct 2026, i.e. around spring 2024; one search summary labelled the talk "GDC 2026", which conflicts with that date)
- [DATA] King's data showed that the maximum recommended level length depends on difficulty: "if a level is really hard, it should also be really short." — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)
- [DESIGNER] King "constantly prunes" its content by fixing its least-fun levels. Guardiola said making a level easier means fewer conversions but "saves players from churning," and those players may spend in later levels. He advised designers to expect long A/B tests. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)
- [DESIGNER, single search snippet, unverified] The same talk reportedly said that the notoriously hard level 65 was originally meant to be the game's final stage, that A/B tests showed higher difficulty raised short-term conversions, and that King measures difficulty separately from a "puzzliness" score used to find the most fun levels. A second search could not confirm "puzzliness" or the level-65 detail. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)
- [DESIGNER, GDC Europe 2016] Jeremy Kang (King) named four level-design pillars: difficulty, rhythm, flow and hooks. "Rhythm" means varying the experience to keep it entertaining. King combines "a designer's sense of fun and hard data from analytics," and Kang cited level 65 as a level that data-driven tuning made more enjoyable. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/king-on-finding-the-fun-in-candy-crush)
- [ANALYST] Candy Crush episodes contain 15 levels, released 3–4 episodes per update. — [Cubix blog](https://www.cubix.co/blog/how-many-levels-are-in-candy-crush-saga/)

**Royal Match (Dream Games)**
- [ANALYST, Anton Slashcev / Gamigion, one analyst's level-by-level reading of levels 1–100] The first 100 levels form 5 worlds of 20 levels. Nine bonus "victory lap" levels appear every 10th level from 20 to 100. Normal levels average about 1.2 attempts with 23–30 moves. Hard levels at 39, 45, 49, 55, 65, 75, 85 and 95 average 1.6 attempts. Super-hard peaks at 59, 69, 79, 89 and 99 take up to 2.5 attempts. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANALYST] The rhythm inside each world: a new mechanic arrives with 2–3 learning boards, then a hard spike, sometimes a super-hard crescendo, then an instant-win bonus level. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANALYST] Move budgets tighten over the campaign: 30–38 moves in levels 1–20, 25–30 in levels 21–60 and 23–27 in levels 61–100. Gamigion estimates this raises decision density by about 20%. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANALYST, conflicting] Funconomy says Royal Match "typically includes 2 hard levels per 10." Gamigion's sample implies about 0.8 hard levels per 10 across levels 1–100. The two may cover different level ranges or count differently. — [Funconomy](https://www.funconomy.com/post/unpacking-royal-kingdom-s-design); contradicted by [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANALYST] Royal Match flags levels as "Hard" or "Super Hard," yet even super-hard levels can be won with 15 moves left thanks to power-up combinations. — [Naavik](https://naavik.co/deep-dives/royal-match/)
- [ANALYST] The bonus level every 10 levels pays out the coins collected and works as a breather after hard levels. — [ironSource LevelUp](https://medium.com/ironsource-levelup/design-deep-dive-02-royal-match-948f7af96f04)
- [ANALYST, 2021] Bonus levels were a rare addition to the genre, and the ability to combine new obstacles into unique levels is "a critical part of making a puzzle game feel fun." — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey)

**Academic and industry data on difficulty and engagement**
- [DATA, CHI PLAY 2014] Linehan et al. analysed play-through videos of Portal, Portal 2 co-op, Braid and Lemmings. In all four, core skills are introduced one at a time: first in simple puzzles, then in puzzles that practise them and combine them with earlier skills, with difficulty rising until the next skill appears. The authors note that the pace of introducing challenges has long been seen as a key determinant of enjoyment, difficulty and learning. — [UCC CORA](https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/3461); [UCC research portal](https://research.ucc.ie/en/publications/learning-curves-analysing-pace-and-challenge-in-four-successful-p/)
- [DATA, IEEE CoG 2019] In the word-puzzle game *Elimination*, an evolutionary level generator was designed to produce a sawtooth difficulty curve. Playtraces from almost 1,000 players confirmed the sawtooth shape. The authors say the sudden drop gives players "breathing room" after a hard run and helps keep them in flow. — [arXiv 1905.06379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06379); [CoG paper](https://ieee-cog.org/2019/papers/paper_239.pdf)
- [ANECDOTE, no data given] A blog claims each sawtooth "tooth" usually spans 5–15 levels in modern puzzle games and credits King's internal research for retention gains, but it publishes no data. — [Ludaxis blog](https://www.ludaxis.io/blog/level-design-puzzle-games-guide)
- [DATA, CHI 2017] Lomas et al. ran three experiments covering more than 20,000 play sessions of an online educational game. When difficulty was assigned at random, the easiest games were the most motivating. Moderately difficult levels were the most motivating only when players chose them. Moderate novelty worked best, and suspense in close games helped. — [CMU HCII news](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games); [Unpaywall/DOI](https://unpaywall.org/10.1145%2F3025453.3025638)
- [DATA, pre-registered, n = 311] An AI-controlled difficulty study found that different difficulty-skill ratios had no significant effect on enjoyment or engagement. — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9890114/)
- [DATA, EA, about 2017] A dynamic-difficulty system for match-3 that treats churn as an "absorbing state" to avoid produced up to 9% better player engagement across several Electronic Arts games. — [Xue et al., ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322414816_Dynamic_Difficulty_Adjustment_for_Maximized_Engagement_in_Digital_Games)
- [DATA, Rovio, 2020] Rovio predicted per-level pass rate and churn for *Angry Birds Dream Blast* with AI agents, using ground truth from 95,266 players. Churn was defined as stopping for at least 7 days after trying a level at least once. Pass rate was the mean of 1/(attempts needed to pass). A 2021 follow-up that combined deep RL with MCTS predicted pass rates more accurately. — [arXiv 2008.12937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12937); [arXiv 2107.12061](https://arxiv.org/html/2107.12061v1)
- [DATA, Tactile Games / Lily's Garden, 2021] A negative binomial distribution fits the per-level distribution of moves for 85% of levels (thesis figure). The attempts metric "has been linked to player churn," but averages depend on which cohort reaches a level, so raw averages can mislead designers. — [arXiv 2107.03305](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.03305); [Kristensen PhD thesis](https://pure.itu.dk/files/109068185/PhD_Thesis_Final_version_Jeppe_Kristensen.pdf); [arXiv 2401.17436](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.17436)
- [ANALYST] Hard "pinch" levels meant to push spending only work if players are tenacious. Without that patience they cause rage-quits and force designers to ease levels later. — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/rethinking-progression-in-mobile-puzzle-games)
- [ANALYST, Feb 2025] Hybrid-casual puzzles succeed with "fast, easy gameplay with just the right amount of challenge" and "fewer levers to pull," so design effort goes into puzzle balance. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/2/3/hybridcasual-puzzles-expanding-the-puzzle-market)
- [ANALYST, Jan 2026] Block puzzles are shifting toward goal-based levels with varying challenge, reframing the genre as "beatable content." — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2026/1/19/from-tetris-to-block-blast-why-block-puzzles-never-stop-printing)

**Premium puzzle games**
- [DESIGNER, GDC Europe 2014] Ken Wong's talk "Designing Monument Valley: Less Game, More Experience" describes trading challenging puzzles for an aesthetic journey, and keeping that journey "short but sublime," which won the game new audiences. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1021380/Designing-Monument-Valley-Less)
- [DESIGNER] Wong: "The final idea that we had to let go of is that a longer game is a better game." The catharsis of finishing was strong enough that "we had to help more people complete it," and for many older players Monument Valley was the first game they had ever completed. — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/monument-valley-designer-games-must-escape-the-trope-of-violence) (attribution among the PocketGamer.biz pages returned by the search is not fully certain)
- [DATA, achievement proxy, biased toward completionists] On Steam, 97.3% of 4,179 qualified players completed Monument Valley's main storyline and 75.9% completed the *Ida's Dream* chapter; median completion time is 2h 55m. On Google Play, achievement rates fall from about 96% for early chapters to about 17–19% for later ones (for example "Completed The Chasm" at 18.69%). — [Steam Hunters](https://steamhunters.com/apps/1927720/achievements); [Exophase](https://www.exophase.com/game/monument-valley-android/achievements/)
- [ANECDOTE] Stephen's Sausage Roll is praised for drawing "layers of deep puzzling" out of very few elements, with an uncompromising but fair difficulty curve that is hard from the very first puzzle. This is the opposite of the casual ramp. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen%27s_Sausage_Roll); [Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/features/10-years-of-grilling-stephens-sausage-roll-remains-one-of-the-most-influential-puzzle-games-ever-created/)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, sawtooth:** plot solver-estimated difficulty (optimal moves, branching, dead-end ratio) for all 300 levels in order. Each world should repeat the cycle new element → 2–3 easy learning levels → rising difficulty → one spike → a breather. Royal Match's teardown puts spikes around level x5/x9 within each block of ten and a bonus or easy level at x0. A monotonic ramp, or random noise from the generator, is a red flag.
- **Audit check, hard means short:** the 39-move ceiling should not sit on the hardest levels. Following King, the hardest levels should have short optimal solutions, so an aha costs few moves to execute, and long 30–39-move levels should be low in insight difficulty. Pet Rescue has no fail state, so "attempts" become restarts, hint uses and time-to-solve; track time-to-abandon against time-to-pass per level, as King does.
- **Audit check, attempt targets:** for a no-fail, no-lives game, a sensible proxy for Royal Match's 1.2/1.6/2.5 attempts is the restart count per solve, kept near 0–1 on normal levels and about 2 on spike levels (inference from the Royal Match figures, not a measured standard for this genre).
- The evidence that players engage most with easy assigned difficulty (Lomas) fits a YouTube Playables audience that never chose this game's difficulty. Lean easier than a premium thinky game by default, and keep Stephen's Sausage Roll-level challenge optional.
- The Monument Valley achievement drop-off (about 96% early to under 20% late on Android) shows that even a short, gentle premium puzzle game loses most players before the end. Front-load the best content, and do not hold the best mechanics back for the last third of 300 levels.

### Gaps
- No published per-level pass-rate or churn targets from King, Dream Games, Playrix or Playdots were found. The Royal Match figures come from one analyst. The GDC "Sugary Statistics" slides and exact metric definitions were not accessible.
- No data was found on the ideal length of a sawtooth "tooth" (the 5–15-level claim is unsourced) or on breather frequency for no-fail Sokoban-likes. Cosmic Express and A Monster's Expedition pacing data was not found.

## 2. What makes a single puzzle satisfying, and why generated puzzles can feel bad (and how to curate them)

### Takeaway
Respected puzzle designers build each level around one required interaction or insight, often working backward from a "cool" solution. They then spend much of their testing time removing unintended, uninteresting alternative solutions. Good puzzles make the goal obvious and the method non-obvious, often with a "catch" where the obvious path fails. Research shows Sokoban difficulty is driven by state-space structure (box count, congestion, dead ends) more than by raw solution length, so solver-based scoring should use structural features. The main risk with generated levels is that they come out solvable but unintentional: there is no "aha," only bookkeeping.

### Cited Findings
**Designer principles**
- [DESIGNER] Alan Hazelden (A Monster's Expedition) starts each puzzle by picking "a single interaction, or chain of interactions, that I want you to have to do as part of the solution." — [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-relaxing-open-world-puzzle-design-of-i-a-monster-s-expedition-i-)
- [DESIGNER, 2024] Hazelden wanted raft travel to be discovered late in early builds, to produce an "I could have been doing that the whole time" moment. — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/2024/07/19/draknek-interview-lok-digital-thinky-puzzle-games-sokobond-express-mobile-steam-deck-apple-arcade/)
- [ANECDOTE, review] A Monster's Expedition hands you "a new mechanic and make[s] you think you discovered it yourself," and each chain of islands seems to contain its own revelation. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/a-monsters-expedition-deserves-a-place-alongside-the-puzzle-game-greats/)
- [DESIGNER] The game was prototyped in PuzzleScript. Its hand-built open-world areas often needed "dozens of iterations" to fit together without issues. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Monster%27s_Expedition); [Thinkycon 2024](https://thinkygames.com/thinkycon/day-1)
- [DESIGNER] Arvi Teikari (Baba Is You): every level "would at least try to incorporate some new interaction or feature." "A very large part of the game's testing process has been eliminating alternative solutions that aren't interesting enough." Levels often started from a "cool" or "amusing" solution, and he then worked out how the player would reach it. Areas spotlight one new rule, with basic words as the "supporting cast." — [Indie Game Website, 2019](https://www.indiegamewebsite.com/2019/07/19/baba-is-you-developer-arvi-teikari-talks-indie-innovation-influences-and-puzzle-design/); [Red Bull](https://www.redbull.com/int-en/baba-is-you-interview); [MCV/Develop](https://mcvuk.com/development-news/when-we-made-baba-is-you/) (which quote comes from which interview was not verified)
- [DESIGNER, GDC Europe 2011] Jonathan Blow frames solving a puzzle as the player saying "I understand." He contrasts this with the "I finally figured it out" style built on arbitrary steps, intricate sequences, red herrings and obtuse mechanisms, and describes the goal as simulating epiphany. Braid and The Witness teach mechanics quickly with introductory puzzles, then explore combinations. — [Game Developer video](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-jon-blow-on-the-truth-in-game-design-); [Amara transcript](https://amara.org/videos/LaGKzzSXnuQl/en-gb/3101350); [VICE Q&A](https://creators.vice.com/en_us/article/eza9da/truth-arising-from-the-system-qa-with-jonathan-blow-of-ithe-witnessi-and-ibraidi)
- [DESIGNER, paraphrase] Game Maker's Toolkit ("What Makes a Good Puzzle?"): a good puzzle makes it obvious what you have to do but not how. A favoured device is the "catch," where a seemingly easy path to the solution turns out to be wrong. — [ResetEra thread](https://www.resetera.com/threads/what-makes-a-good-puzzle-game-makers-toolkit.29537/); [critpoints review](https://critpoints.net/2016/06/09/game-makers-toolkit-review/)
- [ANECDOTE, design writing] An "aha" needs an uncommon insight that effort alone cannot produce, often overcoming functional fixedness. Repeated rule-application solving (sudoku-style) turns into "vigilant bookkeeping" rather than aha. — [The Codex](https://thecodex.ca/puzzles-aha-vs-process/)
- [ANECDOTE] Stephen's Sausage Roll is praised for "teasing out layers of deep puzzling… from so few puzzle elements." — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen%27s_Sausage_Roll)

**Measuring and curating puzzles (procedural / solver-driven)**
- [DATA, AIIDE ~2016] Kartal, Sohre and Guy generated Sokoban levels with MCTS and ran a user study, using TrueSkill to rate puzzle difficulty. Pearson correlations with perceived difficulty on computer-generated levels were: box count r ≈ 0.48, congestion v2 ≈ 0.43, congestion v1 ≈ 0.41. Across all levels: congestion v2 ≈ 0.32, 3×3-block count ≈ 0.24, box count ≈ 0.23. Tile mixing and goal distance were weak or negative. The final evaluation function combined box count, 3×3 blocks and congestion, and a second user study confirmed that higher scores tracked higher perceived difficulty. — [AAAI/AIIDE PDF](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12859/12706/16375); [UMN PDF](https://motion.cs.umn.edu/pub/SokobanMCTS/DataDrivenSokobanMCTS.pdf)
- [DATA, 2010] Jarušek and Pelánek collected human data on Sokoban: 2,000 problems solved over 785 hours. Exact solution length had only a Spearman correlation of about 0.47 with human solve time (secondary figure). 73.8% of game configurations are dead (unsolvable), yet humans spend only about 14.5% of their time in dead states. — [sokoban.dk PDF](http://sokoban.dk/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Difficulty-Rating-of-Sokoban-Puzzle.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220774696_Difficulty_Rating_of_Sokoban_Puzzle)
- [DATA] Pelánek's transport-puzzle study: difficulty differences "are caused mainly by the structure of a problem's state space and cannot be easily captured by static metrics like size of the state space or the length of a solution." — [FLAIRS paper](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/flairs-transport.pdf)
- [DATA, IJCAI 2019] Bento, Pereira and Lelis generated Sokoban initial states with hardness metrics based on pattern-database heuristics and novelty-guided search. By solver effort, the results were harder than levels made by human experts. — [IJCAI](https://www.ijcai.org/proceedings/2019/646)
- [DATA, Tactile, CoG 2020] For *Lily's Garden*, the move count of an RL agent's best ~5% of runs on a level was the best predictor of real players' completion rate (summaries report about 900,000 player attempts over 120 levels; secondary). A weaker agent trained on a one-step curriculum correlated better with humans than the most proficient agent, which appeared to memorize levels. — [arXiv 2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)
- [DATA] Rovio uses AI-agent play plus a player-population model to predict pass rate and churn before release (see section 1). — [arXiv 2008.12937](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12937)
- [DESIGNER] King prunes and fixes its least-fun levels using the time-to-abandon / time-to-pass metric. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)
- [DATA] A generator can be targeted at a designed difficulty shape (sawtooth) and validated against about 1,000 players' traces. — [arXiv 1905.06379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06379)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, one required idea per level:** for each generated level, run the solver with each mechanic or companion ability disabled in turn (an ablation test). If the level is still solvable without the companion it "teaches" (puppy push, frog hop, duck swim, mole dig), the companion is decorative and the level should be rejected or re-tagged. Teikari's and Hazelden's practice amounts to "the intended interaction must be necessary."
- **Audit check, kill the boring alternative solutions:** count the distinct solution routes (or the near-optimal solutions within par + k moves). Many distinct short solutions mean no aha. One forced key move plus some freedom elsewhere is the target. Also check that a greedy or shortest-path baseline (move straight toward the goal, push anything in the way) fails. That failure is the GMTK "catch," and it matters doubly because tap-to-move pathfinding makes the obvious route trivial to try.
- **Audit check, score structure, not length:** solution length alone is a weak predictor of difficulty (ρ ≈ 0.47). Score levels by boulder count, congestion (box-path interactions), number of irreversible actions (pushes into corners, crumbling bridges, digging, ice slides) and dead-state density. Use 12–39 moves as an execution-cost cap, not a difficulty proxy.
- **Audit check, clutter and red herrings:** count elements that are never touched in the optimal solution. A few can stage the "catch," but many unused gates, plates or burrows read as random generator noise. Blow's critique of red herrings suggests keeping them rare and intentional.
- **Audit check, aha placement:** in each world, hand-pick or hand-tune a few showcase levels (for example the first use of a new combination) rather than shipping 100% generated content. King prunes its worst levels; the cheap equivalent here is a ranked pass that removes the bottom 10–20% by score.
- A solver tuned to "solvable and hard" can make levels hard by tedium (long, many boulders) rather than by insight. The Lily's Garden result (the weaker agent's best runs predicted humans best) suggests estimating human difficulty with a bounded or greedy agent rather than the optimal solver.

### Gaps
- No studio postmortem was found describing how a hyper-casual or "escape/rescue" puzzle studio filters generated levels. The criticism that "generated puzzles feel worse" found only anecdotal comments (itch.io game-jam threads), not controlled studies.
- The full texts on solution uniqueness and the effect of "red herrings" on enjoyment could not be retrieved.

## 3. Onboarding and teaching without text (FTUE, first win, first-session length)

### Takeaway
Large A/B studies show that explicit tutorials barely help simple, discoverable games. They raised play time by up to 29% only in the most complex of three games tested, and gave no significant gain in the two simpler ones. Strong puzzle games teach one skill at a time through simple, near-forced levels and then combine skills. Hybrid-casual hits drop the player straight into play with no tutorial taps. No hard benchmark for "seconds to first win" was found, and vendor claims such as "D1 is decided in the first 90 seconds" are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, CHI 2012] Andersen et al. tested 8 tutorial designs across 3 games with more than 45,000 players. Tutorials raised play time by up to 29% in the most complex game and gave no significant engagement gain in the two simpler games. Tutorial investment "may not be justified for games with mechanics that can be discovered through experimentation." — [UW GRAIL PDF](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/The-impact-of-tutorials-on-games-of-varying-Andersen-O'Rourke/d4a00da8cab8c5b2777385a698b02edc69736bea)
- [DATA, 2020] A study of mobile first-time experience found that having a tutorial positively affected **non-expert** players' flow and continuous-use intentions. — [MTI 4(3):41](https://doi.org/10.3390/mti4030041)
- [DATA, pilot, 2022] A pilot study of implicit (in-level) tutorials. — [Heliyon / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9676530/)
- [DATA, CHI PLAY 2014] Portal, Braid and Lemmings introduce skills separately through simple puzzles first, then practice, then combination with earlier skills. — [UCC CORA](https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/3461)
- [DESIGNER] Braid and The Witness teach mechanics quickly with introductory puzzles, then explore combinations. — [Amara transcript](https://amara.org/videos/LaGKzzSXnuQl/en-gb/3101350)
- [ANECDOTE, reviews] A Monster's Expedition teaches log handling "almost via osmosis." Reviewers praise "the assurance with which the designers introduce the nonobvious" and the feeling of having discovered mechanics yourself. — [Edge via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20200521/283016876892016); [wurb.com](https://www.wurb.com/stack/?p=6254); [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/a-monsters-expedition-deserves-a-place-alongside-the-puzzle-game-greats/)
- [DESIGNER] Baba Is You needed "quite a few levels to teach those more basic 'supporting' words first" before spotlighting new rules. — [Indie Game Website](https://www.indiegamewebsite.com/2019/07/19/baba-is-you-developer-arvi-teikari-talks-indie-innovation-influences-and-puzzle-design/)
- [ANALYST] Royal Match introduces each new mechanic with 2–3 learning boards. Its four boosters unlock at levels 8, 14, 17 and 19, and new areas open at levels 1, 10, 21, 41, 61 and 81. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANECDOTE, fan wiki] Candy Crush brings in early features one at a time (for example, level 2 introduces one-layer frosting). — [Candy Crush Wiki](https://candycrush.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Levels_with_new_things)
- [ANALYST, 2025] Hybrid-casual puzzle hits have "no tutorials or extra taps required before starting a level, so players can start playing immediately." — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/2/3/hybridcasual-puzzles-expanding-the-puzzle-market)
- [ANECDOTE, vendor blog, unsourced] "D1 retention is largely decided in the first 90 seconds of the first session." — [VMobify](https://vmobify.com/blog/app-retention-strategy)
- [DESIGNER] Monument Valley deliberately helped more people finish; for many older players it was "the first game they've ever completed." — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/monument-valley-designer-games-must-escape-the-trope-of-violence)
- [ANECDOTE] Because onboarding happens once, "juicing it has little effect on the overall experience"; polish moment-to-moment events instead. — [Wikipedia: Game feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel) and related juice write-ups (attribution among the sources the search returned is uncertain)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, level 1:** the first level should be winnable by a non-gamer in a few taps without reading anything. It should be the shortest in the game (well under the 12-move floor if possible, or at least the simplest 12-move layout), with the cage and home tile visible on one screen and only one plausible path. The Andersen result supports keeping the text-free approach, because Pet Rescue's verbs (move, push, hop, swim, dig) are discoverable through experimentation.
- **Audit check, first appearance of each companion or hazard:** the first level with a new ability should be "forced": the only route requires exactly one use of the new verb, in a spot where the player cannot miss it. The next 1–2 levels should use it again in a slightly different context, and a combination with an older verb should come before any spike (the Royal Match "2–3 learning boards" and Linehan pattern).
- **Audit check, textless signalling:** with no text, every interactive element needs an unambiguous visual affordance (a hedge reads as diggable, a gap as hoppable, water as swimmable) and a demonstration the first time. The solver-driven hint hand can double as a silent tutorial: show it automatically on a mechanic's first level, then only on demand.
- **Audit check, time to first win:** no published benchmark was found. Given in-feed play on YouTube Playables, aim for the first rescue in roughly 30–60 seconds from load (inference, not data), and measure the drop-off between load, first move and first win.
- Experienced players benefit less from tutorials (MTI 2020 and Andersen). Forced tutorial levels should be very short so skilled players are not bored.

### Gaps
- No quantified benchmark was found for first-session length, time to first win or tutorial completion rate in casual puzzle games (the planned FTUE search was cut off by the search budget). No Voodoo or SayGames guidance on first-level duration was found.

## 4. Game feel / juice: feedback, animation, sound, celebration, and measured impact

### Takeaway
Controlled studies find that juicy feedback reliably raises perceived visual appeal and aesthetic enjoyment, and haptics can improve enjoyment and immersion. The effect on competence is conditional. Exaggerated or redundant feedback can lower players' sense of competence and agency, and one study found players performed worse in the juicier version. Feedback that is contingent on success and that encourages curiosity predicted enjoyment and voluntary play. No public study links juice to retention in mobile puzzle games.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, CHI PLAY 2019, N = 40 per a citing paper] Hicks et al. compared juicy and non-juicy versions of the same games (PXI questionnaire). Visual embellishments improved visual appeal in all games but affected competence only under specific circumstances. — [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Juicy-Game-Design:-Understanding-the-Impact-of-on-Hicks-Gerling/5914c05b99f717e4ada667e1b23630493eabf3ad); [Meiners et al. 2025 (citing, N = 40)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3723498.3723720)
- [DATA, CHI 2024] Kao et al. ran a 2×2 plus control study in an action RPG. Exaggerated feedback **lowered** self-rated competence and effectance. Making success contingent on the feedback raised enjoyment but weakened competence ratings. **Curiosity** was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and playtime, and feedback that varied with success drew more voluntary play. The authors suggest amplified feedback may disrupt the sense of agency. — [ACM DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656); [Imperial Spiral](https://spiral.imperial.ac.uk/entities/publication/253c32f5-d124-4a23-88b5-9aaaf114608d)
- [DATA, 2016 poster] Juul and Begy: players rated a juicy version of a game higher but **performed worse** in it; the hypotheses were only partly supported. — [Elsevier Pure](https://adk.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/good-feedback-for-bad-players-a-preliminary-study-of-juicy-interf)
- [DATA, CHI 2021] Juicy vibrotactile (haptic) embellishments improved enjoyability, aesthetic appeal, immersion and meaning on the PXI. — [U. Waterloo Haptic Experience Lab](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve)
- [DATA, CHI PLAY 2024] Hicks, Rogers, Gerling and Nacke found that juicy-design principles also apply to audio and sound design. — [Staffordshire eprint PDF](https://eprints.staffs.ac.uk/8527/1/3677084.pdf)
- [DESIGNER, 2012] Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho's talk "Juice it or lose it" (adding effects live to a Breakout clone) is the canonical reference for juice. Practitioners stress that juice amplifies a working core loop but cannot rescue a dull one, and that readability is the test: if an effect makes the game state harder to read, dial it back. — [Wikipedia: Game feel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_feel); [valdemird blog](https://valdemird.com/blog/game-feel-on-the-web/)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, contingent celebration:** big celebrations (pet reunion, confetti, star burst, sound sting) should fire on genuine accomplishments: rescue, reaching home, the first use of a companion, an under-par finish. Do not fire them on every tap. Kao's data suggests exaggerated, non-contingent feedback erodes competence and agency.
- **Audit check, readability:** in a logic game, animation must never hide state. Ice slides, current drift, bee patrols and crumbling bridges should animate clearly and fast enough to read (with step-by-step animation that can be skipped), and undo should visibly rewind. Juul and Begy's "rated higher, performed worse" result is a warning for puzzle clarity.
- **Audit check, micro-feedback on every input:** the tap acknowledgement, path preview for tap-to-move, boulder push weight, a "bump" on an invalid move, and haptic ticks where the platform allows. Haptic evidence (CHI 2021) supports optional vibration.
- **Curiosity lever:** curiosity predicted playtime best (Kao). Teasing the next companion, biome or hat on the level-complete screen is likely a stronger "one more level" pull than more particles (inference).

### Gaps
- No published A/B results on juice, celebrations or sound affecting **retention** in mobile casual or puzzle games were found. The UW "Placing a Value on Aesthetics in Online Casual Games" (CHI 2011) results could not be retrieved. Kao et al.'s exact effect sizes were not retrieved because the search budget ran out.

## 5. Hints, undo, skips and frustration management

### Takeaway
Unlimited, instant undo and restart are the norm in respected Sokoban-likes (Baba Is You, Snakebird). They make puzzles less tedious without making them easier. Hints work best when they match the player's actual progress, avoid spoiling the whole solution and feel like help rather than cheating. Progressive, adaptive hints beat rule-based ones in controlled studies. In free-to-play contexts, easing a level after repeated failure trades a little monetization for less churn.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, 2024 PhD thesis, St Andrews] In a Sudoku study, players were less systematic and more error-prone than solver models assumed. A hint system that rates step difficulty with Minimal Unsatisfiable Sets was as helpful as an existing system, improved player experience, felt less like cheating, and was chosen significantly more often across four studies. — [St Andrews repository](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/29069)
- [DATA, 2022] Surveys found most puzzle-game hint systems are rule-based. Many players said hint timing did not match their actual progress, and some hints were too vague or spoiled upcoming puzzles. An adaptive, multimodal hint system significantly improved player experience and difficulty balance in a controlled experiment. — [Hao et al. PDF](https://faculty.washington.edu/weicaics/paper/papers/HaoHZC2022.pdf)
- [ANECDOTE] Many games use progressively stronger hints so players get only as much help as they need. — [Tropedia: Hint System](https://tropedia.fandom.com/wiki/Hint_System)
- [ANECDOTE, blog] Cheap or free hints can undermine a difficulty curve, and directional clues work better than full solutions. — [Level Walks](https://levelwalks.com/blog/mobile-puzzle-games-adults-guide)
- [ANALYST, 2025] Let players advance after a few failed attempts, and make a repeatedly failed level easier so frustration does not turn into churn. — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/2/3/hybridcasual-puzzles-expanding-the-puzzle-market)
- [DESIGNER/DATA] King: making a level easier costs conversions but saves players from churning. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)
- [ANECDOTE, practitioner analysis, not peer-reviewed] In a churn model of a 50,000-user mobile game, level progression was the strongest retention signal (correlation −0.49 with churn), and "hints used" was among the behavioural features. — [Medium](https://medium.com/@sezenhs22/churn-prediction-model-for-a-mobile-game-79a8773254ba)
- [ANECDOTE, design overview] Baba Is You and Snakebird let players undo every action one by one, which shifts the focus from dying to trying new solutions. — [Game Design Skills](https://gamedesignskills.com/game-design/puzzle/)
- [ANECDOTE, community accessibility guide] "Undo doesn't make a puzzle easier, only less tedious, and it's always optional." Undo should work from a fail state ("don't end the level after a fatal mistake"). — [Steam Puzzle Lovers guide](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/puzzlelovers/discussions/0/7340374196882201829/)
- [ANECDOTE] A push-block game developer (PUSH) resisted undo to give decisions weight, but players kept asking for it. — [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/661100/discussions/0/1696045708652400201/)
- [ANECDOTE] Instant restarts drive the "one-more-try" replay loop in browser games. — [dotgg.gg](https://dotgg.gg/the-one-more-try-effect-why-instant-restarts-make-browser-games-so-replayable)
- [ANECDOTE] Undoing a move shows exactly where your logic went wrong, while full restarts erase that feedback. — [okagame blog](https://okagame.com/blog/common-mistakes-puzzle-games-avoid)
- [ANECDOTE] Some A Monster's Expedition players say they could not get all the achievements without a guide, which suggests that even premium "gentle" games strand completionists. — [Steam community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1052990)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, undo:** free, unlimited, single-step and instant undo (including undoing a crumbled bridge, an ice slide, a dig or a current drift, and resetting bee positions) matches best practice. Confirm that undo works after a "stuck" state (pet trapped, boulder cornered) and that the game never forces a full restart.
- **Audit check, hint ladder:** a solver-driven hint hand risks spoiling the whole puzzle if it plays the full solution. Prefer a ladder: (1) highlight the key object or companion, (2) show the next 1–3 moves from the **player's current state** rather than from the start (the 2022 study found progress-matched timing matters), and (3) show the full path only on a third request. Offer hints proactively only after idle time or repeated restarts, and cap or cool them down (or gate later steps behind a rewarded ad) so they do not flatten the curve.
- **Audit check, dead-state detection:** the solver can tell when the current state is unwinnable (73.8% of Sokoban configurations are dead, and humans rarely linger in them). A gentle, textless "undo" pulse when the state becomes dead is a high-value frustration reducer.
- **Audit check, skip:** with 300 levels and no fail state, a skip, or letting players move on after N restarts or minutes (DoF), protects against churn on a single bad generated level. Hint and skip usage per level is also the cheapest signal for pruning bad levels (King-style).
- A hint should not silently cost stars without the player knowing, or feel like cheating. The St Andrews result suggests hints that explain *why* (for example, highlighting the blocking boulder) feel less like cheating than showing the move.

### Gaps
- No published A/B data from major studios on hint frequency, hint cost or hint-usage-to-churn curves was found. No data was found on how long casual players stay stuck before quitting.

## 6. Scoring, stars and par systems: do move-par stars help or hurt casual players?

### Takeaway
The best experimental evidence (UW, more than 27,000 players) shows that secondary objectives extend play for committed players but make many players play less, unless they reinforce the primary goal. Move-par stars partly reinforce the goal (an efficient rescue), but they risk turning a win into a perceived failure. Practitioner critiques fault stars at both extremes: thresholds so loose that three stars are meaningless, or so tight that they frustrate. A 3-star system works best when a puzzle has several distinct solutions of increasing quality.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, FDG 2011] Andersen et al. ran A/B tests on two Flash puzzle games (Refraction and Hello Worlds) with more than 27,000 players, measuring play time, progress and return rate. Optional secondary objectives (coins) "can encourage long-term players to extend their playtime, but they can also cause many players to play for less time." In Refraction, a secondary summary reports that removing coins raised the median number of levels completed from 11 to 17 (not verified against the full paper). Modifying objectives so they reinforce the game's primary goal avoided the harm while keeping long-term players engaged. — [UW GRAIL project page](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/fdg2011); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2159365.2159370)
- [DATA/DESIGN, 2024] Kwon, Shyne and Cooper argue the 3-star system works when a puzzle has many distinct solutions of increasing difficulty. Performance can be measured as higher score, fewer moves, less time or less common approaches, and they generate such puzzles with a genetic algorithm. — [CEUR-WS Vol-4090](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4090/short3.pdf)
- [ANECDOTE, review] Picross 3D uses three separate criteria (finish within a target time, finish with no mistakes, complete the puzzle), and stars unlock bonus puzzles. The reviewer felt this "caters to all skill levels." — [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/23121/picross-3d-nintendo-ds)
- [ANECDOTE, review] Some games use stars to "reward failure"; in one, it was "virtually impossible to earn fewer than three stars" on far too many levels. — [TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/?p=75409)
- [ANECDOTE, review] In Rubek, star thresholds were too generous: the reviewer often earned three stars with about 20% fewer moves than required, sometimes 30%. — [RAWG review](https://rawg.io/games/rubek/reviews)
- [DATA] Lomas et al.: when difficulty is chosen, moderate challenge motivates most. This supports optional rather than mandatory par challenges. — [CMU HCII](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, stars never gate basic progress:** finishing a level at any move count must always advance the campaign and award at least one star. Star-gated content (worlds, garden, hats) should need well below 100% of available stars. Otherwise move-par becomes a mandatory secondary objective of the kind Andersen found harmful to most players.
- **Audit check, threshold tuning:** if three stars equal the solver's optimal move count, a solver-verified par will be very hard for casual players on 30–39-move levels and trivial on 12-move levels. A tiered par (for example 1 star for any finish, 2 stars at optimal + X% (~20–30%), 3 stars at or near optimal) avoids both "meaningless" and "impossible." Check the actual star distribution in playtests. Reviews flag ~20% slack as already "too generous" for enthusiasts, so tighten the slack on short levels.
- **Audit check, tap-to-move vs par:** confirm that par counts tiles moved (or pushes), not taps, and that pathfinding's automatic route is always the shortest one, so tap-to-move users are not penalised.
- **Audit check, framing:** show the par target only after the first completion, or show it softly, so casual players experience a rescue rather than a near-failure. Offer "replay for 3 stars" as an optional loop. Sudden harsh feedback in a textless UI (an empty-star animation) may read as failure.
- Move-par stars reinforce the primary goal better than unrelated collectibles (Andersen's mitigation), because an efficient route deepens understanding of the same puzzle. That is a point in their favour, provided they are optional.

### Gaps
- No studio data (King, Rovio, ZeptoLab) on 3-star completion distributions or on the retention effect of star systems in casual mobile games was found. The Refraction 11→17 figure and the exact play-time percentages need checking against the full FDG 2011 paper (it could not be fetched).

## 7. Variety and novelty: how often new elements must appear; level-count expectations

### Takeaway
Players respond best to moderate novelty. Royal Match's first 100 levels add a new blocker roughly every 8–9 levels (11 by level 91), plus new areas and boosters, and new difficulty comes mostly from recombining and tightening known elements. Premium thinky games aim for every level or island chain to show a new interaction, getting depth from few elements. Live casual leaders ship thousands of levels (Royal Match more than 12,400 by Nov 2025; Candy Crush more than 15,000), while premium and hyper-casual titles range from 10 chapters to about 55 levels to a few hundred.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, CHI 2017] Moderate novelty maximised motivation; too much or too little reduced it. — [CMU HCII](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games)
- [DATA, CHI 2024] Curiosity was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and playtime. — [ACM DOI](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656)
- [ANALYST] Royal Match levels 1–100 add 11 new blocks (levels 1–91), 6 new areas (levels 1, 10, 21, 41, 61, 81), 4 boosters (levels 8, 14, 17, 19) and 3 event rules, with tightening move budgets. — [Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)
- [ANALYST] Royal Match avoids difficulty plateaus by layering obstacles and tightening move limits rather than relying on sudden spikes. — [Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@ekinmelissezer/game-analysis-for-royal-match-and-toon-blast-9c4bff8ef48b)
- [ANALYST, 2021] Combining new obstacles to create unique levels is "a critical part of making a puzzle game feel fun." — [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey)
- [ANECDOTE, fan wiki] Late in Candy Crush's life, new blockers debut at specific episodes (for example Crystal Candy in episode 308 and Order Lock in episode 528), and long stretches reuse existing elements. — [Crystal Candy wiki](https://candycrush.fandom.com/wiki/Crystal_Candy); [Order Lock wiki](https://candycrush.fandom.com/wiki/Order_Lock)
- [DESIGNER] Baba Is You aims for every level to bring in some new interaction. — [Indie Game Website](https://www.indiegamewebsite.com/2019/07/19/baba-is-you-developer-arvi-teikari-talks-indie-innovation-influences-and-puzzle-design/)
- [ANECDOTE] In A Monster's Expedition, each chain of islands seems to contain its own revelation or new interaction. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/a-monsters-expedition-deserves-a-place-alongside-the-puzzle-game-greats/)
- [ANECDOTE] Stephen's Sausage Roll gets its depth from very few elements. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen%27s_Sausage_Roll)
- [DATA, level counts] Royal Match had more than 12,400 levels as of November 2025, with new levels every two weeks. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Match). Candy Crush has shipped more than 15,000 levels. — [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034392/Sugary-Statistics-What-We-Have). Monument Valley has 10 levels/chapters. — [God is a Geek review](https://godisageek.com/?p=143879). The web version of Pull the Pin has 55 levels "in a gently escalating difficulty." — [Lagged](https://lagged.com/en/g/pull-the-pin)
- [ANECDOTE, launch reviews] Two Dots launched with 85 levels of ascending difficulty, and reviewers said you had to be "pretty savvy" to get past the first five or so (a steep early ramp). Forum users complain that some levels are unbeatable even with purchases. — [Tom's Guide review](https://www.tomsguide.com/us/two-dots%2Creview-2201.html); [Digital Trends](https://digitaltrends.com/?p=587606)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, novelty cadence:** list the level where each element first appears: 4 companions (puppy, frog, duck, mole), ice, currents, plates/gates, crumbling bridges, burrows, bees, plus biomes and hats. With about 10–12 mechanics over 300 levels, a new element arrives on average every 25–30 levels, roughly 3× slower than Royal Match's early cadence. Fill the gaps with **new combinations** (frog plus ice, duck plus current, puppy plus plates) and visual novelty (biome, hats, pet species) at least every ~10 levels, and front-load: introduce most elements in the first ~100 levels.
- **Audit check, combination variety:** generated levels in a world should not all use the same 1–2 elements. Measure the element-set diversity of each run of 10 consecutive levels and flag runs that repeat the same composition.
- **Audit check, level count:** 300 levels sits between premium (10–300) and live casual (thousands). With curiosity and novelty as the main drivers, it is better to ship fewer, higher-quality levels with denser novelty than 300 near-duplicates. A daily puzzle pool helps after the campaign.

### Gaps
- No quantified "boredom threshold" (how many levels with the same elements before engagement drops) was found for any puzzle genre. No mechanic-introduction data was found for Cosmic Express, Snakebird or A Monster's Expedition.

## 8. Session-length norms for casual puzzle games and short-form/in-feed play

### Takeaway
Mobile benchmarks put the median session at about 5–6 minutes (top quartile 8–9 minutes, 2024 data). Hyper-casual sessions are shorter, about 4.5–6.7 minutes with about 4–5 sessions a day (2018–19 data). Puzzle is among the strongest genres for retention and playtime, with D1 around 30% by one AppsFlyer-derived figure. YouTube Playables had no public session-length metric until mid-2026, and the widely repeated "~10 minutes per session" target is unsourced advice. Levels should be bite-sized (well under a minute or two) so in-feed players get several completions per session.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, GameAnalytics, 2024 data / 2025 report] Mobile average session length was 8–9 minutes for the top 25% of games, with a median of 5–6 minutes. D1 retention for the top 25% was about 26.5–27.7% (down from 28–29% in 2023) and 10–11.5% for the bottom 25%. On iOS the top 25% reached 31–33% D1, against 25–27% on Android. Puzzle is among the genres with the highest short- and long-term retention, and ranks high on playtime and session count. — [GameDev Reports summary](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks); [2025 GameAnalytics PDF mirror](https://investgame.net/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/2025-GameAnalytics-Mobile-Gaming-Benchmarks.pdf)
- [DATA, GameAnalytics Q1 2024] Puzzle games "consistently perform well across metrics and regions with minimal fluctuation." — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-benchmarks-in-mobile)
- [DATA, GameAnalytics] Games with high session length and retention, such as puzzle, card and casino, "may be more suited to ad-based monetization." — [GameAnalytics Benchmarks Report PDF](https://public-production.gameanalytics.com/assets/GameAnalytics%20Benchmarks%20Report.pdf)
- [DATA, GameAnalytics 2018–19, conflicting definitions] Hyper-casual sessions average 4.5 minutes at about 4.6 sessions a day. A different summary gives a median hyper-casual session of 6m42s, against 14m30s across the industry. — [Game World Observer, 2019](https://gameworldobserver.com/2019/07/02/hyper-casual-167m-sessions-a-year); [GameRefinery](https://www.gamerefinery.com/hyper-casual-vs-idle-the-latest-trends-in-mobile-games/) (which figure comes from which page was not verified)
- [DATA, AppsFlyer-derived, secondary] Puzzle retention: D1 31.85%, D7 12.18%, D30 5.35%. — [Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies). Hyper-casual D1 about 29.31% and D30 about 1.4–2%. — [MAF](https://maf.ad/en/blog/mobile-game-retention-benchmarks/)
- [DATA, GameAnalytics 2026 report on 2025 data, PC for contrast] PC D1 for the top 25% is 15–16%, and median daily playtime is 32–33 minutes. — [GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-and-pc-game)
- [ANALYST, 2026] Until mid-July 2026, YouTube Playables publishers had only one metric per game (total plays); average session time was expected next. — [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-updates)
- [ANECDOTE, guides without a YouTube source] Developer guides repeat a target of about 10 minutes average playtime per session for Playables. Playables users play in short, spontaneous sessions between videos. — [playables.in requirements guide](https://playables.in/youtube-playables-requirements/); [playables.in how-to guide](https://playables.in/how-to-make-games-for-youtube-playables/). Sponsored playables can run 2–5 minutes if the mechanic is sticky. — [Influencers Time](https://www.influencers-time.com/youtube-playables-how-brands-should-brief-sponsored-mini-gam/)
- [DATA] King: very hard levels should be short. — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)

### Inferences
- **Audit check, level duration:** at 12–39 moves, with tap-to-move collapsing several moves into one tap, a typical level should take about 20–90 seconds including thinking. In a 5-minute in-feed session, that gives about 4–10 completed rescues. Each one is a small "win," which feeds the one-more-level pull. Flag any level whose median solve time exceeds about 3 minutes.
- **Audit check, resume and instant start:** in-feed sessions are spontaneous and interruptible. Resume straight to the next unsolved level with no menus. Persist mid-level state if the platform allows. Make "next level" the default, one-tap action on the win screen (the hybrid-casual "no extra taps" principle).
- **Audit check, natural stopping points:** worlds of about 10–20 levels with a bonus or breather at the end give satisfying stopping points and a reason to return (the Royal Match structure). A daily puzzle with a streak adds a return hook that fits short sessions.
- The 10-minute Playables target is unverified advice. Measure the actual session distribution once YouTube's analytics expose it, and do not stretch levels to hit it.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube Playables benchmark for session length, levels per session or retention was found. The YouTube developer documentation (developers.google.com) was unreachable. No authoritative 2025–2026 GameAnalytics session-length figure specific to puzzle games was found; the figures above are all-genre or hyper-casual. No research was retrieved on "one more level" psychology (Zeigarnik effect, auto-advance, cliffhanger reveals) because the search budget ran out. Treat any claims there as unsupported by this research.
