# Audience, Platform Policy and Regulatory Limits on Engagement and Ad Monetization for a Cute, Textless Pet-Rescue Puzzle Game on YouTube Playables

Research date: 18 October 2026. Method note: every finding below comes from web-search result summaries. Direct page fetches failed in this environment: developers.google.com, support.google.com and ico.org.uk were blocked at the proxy. The web-search budget ran out before the last check (the exact name and date of the EU "KIDS Act" proposal). Primary-source wording, especially Google help pages and the ICO and Commission texts, should be checked again before anything is quoted verbatim in a public document. Each item is labelled **[LAW]** (binding statute or regulation), **[PROPOSAL]** (pending legislation), **[PLATFORM]** (contractual platform policy), **[GUIDANCE]** (regulator guidance, not binding by itself), **[SELF-REG]** (industry self-regulation) or **[RESEARCH/INDUSTRY]**.

## Q1. Audience: who plays cute casual puzzle/pet games, who plays YouTube Playables, and how children use YouTube

### Takeaway
The main paying audience for casual puzzle, match-3 and "cozy" games is adult and leans female, often aged 25 and over. Cute, textless, one-finger animal games also draw young children, and those children are already on YouTube in very large numbers, often on the main app (supervised or not), not only in YouTube Kids. Nobody publishes demographics for YouTube Playables players, so a Playables game has to be designed for a mixed audience of adults and children it cannot identify.

### Cited Findings
**Casual and puzzle demographics**
- ESA Global Video Games Report 2025: 24,216 active players (at least 1 hour a week) in 22 countries; 48% identify as female; 62% of women favour puzzle titles vs 39% of men; older players also lean toward puzzle and single-player games; little gender difference in *why* people play — [GameDevReports summary of ESA 2025](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/esa-global-video-games-report-2025)
- Anzu 2025, cited by Gameloft: nearly half of all gamers are female, and mobile reaches over 80% of players worldwide — [Gameloft for Brands](https://www.gameloft.com/for-brands/news/unlocking-the-2025-gaming-audience)
- Sensor Tower, via PocketGamer.biz: more than 50% of mobile gamers are women, and they generate nearly 30% of mobile game revenue — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/games-favoured-by-women-set-to-exceed-16-billion-in-revenue-this-year)
- Sensor Tower title-level example: one casual title's US audience was 67% female in 4Q23, and "casual titles typically skew more female" — [Sensor Tower blog (Merge Gardens vs Gardenscapes)](https://sensortower-china.com/blog/merge-gardens-advertising-underpenetrated-on-you-tube-vs-gardenscapes)
- Chartboost/Newzoo (US): the mobile games audience is mostly women over 25 (headline: "women make up 62% of the mobile games market") — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/women-make-up-62-of-the-mobile-games-market)
- Quantic Foundry (2017, about 270,000 gamers, self-selected, mostly recruited via Facebook): Match-3 and Family/Farm Sim player bases are each 69% female, the highest of any genre. The figure is the female share of each genre's players, not the share of women who play that genre — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/study-shows-which-video-game-genres-women-play-most-1791435415); [Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/?p=7846). Coverage of the same study puts casual puzzle at about 42% female, but this was not confirmed against the original — [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2017/01/21/study-breaks-down-game-genre-playerbases-by-gender)
- Older casual-game research (2006 PopCap survey, dated and opt-in): 76% female; 71% aged 40+ and 47% aged 50+ — [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/survey-popcap-releases-casual-game-findings)
- Puzzle led the casual market in 2025 in both revenue ($8.25bn, +7.6% YoY) and downloads (7.7bn, +9.9% YoY), with Match-3 the largest subgenre. These are AppMagic figures, reported in a mobilegamer.biz data digest; the exact source page was not confirmed — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/data-digest-kingshot-outpaces-whiteout-survival-female-players-overlooked-casual-market-flat-januarys-top-titles-more/)

**YouTube Playables audience (no official demographics)**
- Playables opened to all signed-in users in May 2024 with 75+ games — [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables); [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/49745-youtubes-playables-a-strategic-move-to-engage-casual-gamers)
- Trade press reports that Playables has quietly expanded to more than 50 markets, including the EU and the Nordics. YouTube has not published a country list — [mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/youtube-has-quietly-rolled-out-playables-across-europe/)
- No company, YouTube included, has published a monthly active user figure for Playables. Publisher Playgama claims about 57 million *plays* (not users) in June 2026 across roughly 50 games — [Shattered.io](https://shattered.io/youtube-playables-50-markets-zero-iap-2026/)
- Unofficial trackers report more than 1,970 games and 5.31 billion total plays as of October 2026 — [playables.online](https://playables.online/). Mediacube reports over 1 billion plays added between 1 July and 4 September 2026, with the catalogue growing from 788 to 1,477 titles; session analytics cover the US, Australia, Canada, UK, India and Malaysia — [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en-US/blog/youtube-playables-updates)

**How children use YouTube**
- Pew Research (survey of 3,054 parents of children 12 and under, 13–26 May 2025, published October 2025): 85% say their child watches YouTube; among parents of children under 2, the share rose from 45% (2020) to 62% — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/report-more-parents-say-their-kids-under-2-watch-youtube-than-in-2020/); [PSB Insights](https://www.psbinsights.com/work/pew-research-center-how-parents-manage-screen-time-for-kids/)
- Ofcom's Children and Parents: Media Use and Attitudes 2025 covers ages 3–17. Secondary summaries say about 89% of 3–17-year-olds play games on at least one device. No 2025 YouTube-specific share was found; Ofcom's 2021 data had YouTube used by 89% of children — [Ofcom 2025](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-children/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025); [Computing at School summary](https://www.computingatschool.org.uk/forum-news-blogs/2025/may/understanding-media-use-among-children-aged-3-11-key-insights-from-ofcom-s-2025-media-literacy-report/)
- YouTube offers three routes for children. YouTube Kids is a separate app for under-13s. Supervised child accounts are a parent-managed version of main YouTube for children under 13 (or the local age). Supervised teen accounts are a voluntary option, typically for ages 13–17 — [YouTube Help: supervised child account](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10314940); [YouTube Help: supervised teen account](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15253498); [Google for Families](https://support.google.com/families/answer/10495678)
- Google's Family Link guidance warns that "some features on YouTube aren't currently available for children under 13". It does not say whether Playables is one of them — [Family Link guidebook](https://guidebooks.google.com/family-link/manage-apps/youtube-kids?hl=en-us)
- From 13 August 2025, YouTube began rolling out AI age estimation in the US. It infers whether a user is under 18 from viewing history, content categories and account age, ignoring the stated birthdate. Users flagged as teens get non-personalized ads, "take a break" and bedtime reminders on by default, and other protections. YouTube warned creators of a possible ad-revenue decrease "since we only serve non-personalized ads to those viewers" — [Variety](https://au.variety.com/2025/digital/news/youtube-ai-age-verification-automatically-restrict-under-18-users-26503); [Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/youtube-age-estimation-us-rollout-3586621)

### Inferences
- The revenue base (adult, female-leaning casual players) and the at-risk audience (young children drawn to cute, textless, one-finger animal play) overlap in the same game. Because the game is textless and one-finger, a non-reading child aged 3–7 can play it without help.
- On Playables the developer cannot see age. YouTube knows it (supervised, teen or age-estimated accounts) and applies its own ad and wellbeing rules. The design therefore has to be safe for the youngest plausible player and still appeal to adults.
- Design priorities that follow: wording-free clarity (already a strength), short sessions with natural stopping points, no reliance on reading for disclosures (ad and reward cues must be iconographic and unambiguous), and no mechanics whose pull depends on anxiety or loss.

### Gaps
- No published age, gender or region breakdown for YouTube Playables players; no official Playables MAU.
- No reliable demographic data specifically for pet or animal puzzle games. The female and adult skew is inferred from casual, match-3 and decoration genre data.
- Whether Playables appear for supervised child accounts, supervised teen accounts or YouTube Kids could not be confirmed. YouTube Kids is very unlikely to carry Playables, given the Playables "not made for kids" rule below, but no source states it directly.
- No 2025 Ofcom YouTube usage percentage for ages 3–17.

## Q2. YouTube and Google platform policies: Made for Kids, Playables audience rules, supervised accounts, and Google Play Families as a comparable standard

### Takeaway
YouTube's own Playables Trust & Safety rules say a Playable MUST NOT be specifically directed at children or labelled "made for kids" or "kids only", and must suit a general audience aged 13+. Ad content in Playables must also be 13+. A cute pet-rescue game therefore has to be positioned and built as an all-ages, general-audience game. It cannot be a kids' game. YouTube controls the ads, and the Families and AdMob policies are the best public benchmark for "child-safe" ad behaviour.

### Cited Findings
**YouTube Playables requirements [PLATFORM]**
- Trust & Safety requirement: Playables content "MUST NOT" be specifically directed at children or be "made for kids" or "kids only"; it must suit a general audience aged 13+; Playables ad content "MUST" suit a general audience (13+); games must follow the YouTube Community Guidelines — [Playables Trust & Safety requirements (EN)](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_trustsafety); [Spanish version, quoted in the search result](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_trustsafety?hl=es-419)
- The requirements use RFC 2119 keywords. "MUST NOT" is an absolute prohibition, and a Playable that fails any such requirement "will not be allowed to publish on YouTube"; all Playables are reviewed before public launch — [Playables certification requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements)
- The SDK's ads namespace is a "public preview" API and subject to change — [Playables SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/sdk)
- Secondary guides, not confirmed against Google: third-party ad SDKs such as AdMob, Unity Ads or AppLovin cannot be integrated, and ads run through YouTube's own infrastructure. Off-platform monetization (payment links, QR codes) is restricted. Audio mute/unmute and pause/resume must be handled. Ads must be enabled in the Developer Portal — [Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/main/youtube-playables-monetization/); [Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-monetization); [playables.in](https://playables.in/youtube-playables-developer-portal/)
- A secondary guide says the certification suite checks that rewarded-ad requests complete their full cycle, and that a rewarded button which yields no ad fails certification (unconfirmed) — [Mediacube: certification errors](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-errors)
- Trade press reports Playables have zero in-app purchases as of 2026 — [Shattered.io](https://shattered.io/youtube-playables-50-markets-zero-iap-2026/)

**Made for Kids (MFK) designation on YouTube [PLATFORM, driven by LAW]**
- MFK content gets no personalized ads. Comments, notifications, autoplay on home, end screens, the notification bell, Save to playlist and other features are restricted to comply with COPPA. YouTube warns that this "may result in a decrease in revenue for some creators" — [YouTube Help (via yt.be)](https://yt.be/help/9Sgi); [YouTube Help: Determining if your content is made for kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076); [MFK FAQ](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9684541)
- Origin: introduced in January 2020 after the $170 million FTC and New York AG COPPA settlement with Google/YouTube — [Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2020/01/07/youtube-disables-more-product-features-kid-oriented-content/)
- Ads shown to supervised accounts and on MFK content must follow YouTube's MFK ad policy — [YouTube Help: How ads work for supervised accounts and MFK content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9713557)
- YouTube Kids ad policy bans interest-based advertising and bans ads for video games whose rating would not suit audiences aged 12 and under — [YouTube Help: Advertising on YouTube Kids](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6168681)
- FTC guidance to YouTube creators: content is not "directed to children" just because some children may see it. The factors are subject matter, visual content, animated characters, child-oriented activities and incentives, age of models, and language — [FTC business blog, Nov 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children). YouTube treats content as "mixed audience" when children are not the primary audience but the content still targets them — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076)

**Google Play Families / AdMob as comparable standards [PLATFORM, not binding on Playables]**
- Families policy (preview page): banned formats include "monetization and advertising that interfere with normal app use or game play, including rewarded or opt-in ads, that are not closeable after 5 seconds" and interstitials "displayed immediately upon app launch". Non-interfering ads may run longer — [Play Console Help: Families policies preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17122218)
- Families: monetization in child-directed apps must not be deceptive or designed to cause inadvertent clicks by children. Ad networks must not use a dismiss button that triggers another ad — [Google Play Families](https://goo.gle/families)
- General Play Ads policy: full-screen interstitials not closeable after 15 seconds are banned (opt-in rewarded excepted); video interstitials before the splash screen are banned; ads may not imitate app UI or OS notifications — [Play Console Help: Ads](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)
- Apps serving children or users of unknown age may only use Families self-certified ad SDKs — [Families Self-Certified Ads SDK Policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/12918983); [Policy updates](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14550832)
- Developer reports show Families rejections over close buttons hidden by an SDK "UI fade" default, and over rewarded ads with invisible close buttons — [Unity Discussions](https://discussions.unity.com/t/google-rejected-my-game-for-having-ads-not-closeable-after-5-sec/899959)
- AdMob rewarded ads policy: "clear, accurate and conspicuous disclosure of the action(s) required and reward(s) offered prior to each instance". Bundles ("watch 3 ads") must be disclosed. Rewarded ads may be served only after the user "affirmatively and unambiguously opts in", and must not oblige users to interact with the ad — [AdMob Help: Rewarded ads policy](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578)
- AdMob rewarded interstitials (no opt-in) require an intro screen that announces the reward and offers a clear opt-out; only skippable ads are served — [AdMob Help: Rewarded interstitial](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/9884467)

### Inferences
- The binding constraint for this game is the Playables "not made for kids, 13+ general audience" rule. On the FTC factors (animated animals, child-oriented rescue activity, textless one-finger play) the game already leans child-appealing. Its positioning (store art, title, description, marketing, any YouTube channel promotion) should be clearly all-ages or "cozy for everyone", avoiding "for kids", nursery or toddler framing. Labelling it "for kids" would both break Playables certification and invite COPPA child-directed treatment.
- Because YouTube serves the ads, personalisation, ad-category filtering and age handling sit with YouTube. The developer controls only *when* and *how* ads are requested and how the reward is presented. That is where the audit should focus.
- The Families 5-second and no-launch-interstitial rules, and the AdMob opt-in and disclosure rules, are not binding on Playables. They are the best public statement of what Google itself considers child-safe ad behaviour, and a good benchmark for a mixed audience.

### Gaps
- Exact English wording of the Playables Trust & Safety page, and any Playables-specific rules on interstitial frequency, placement or rewarded-ad disclosure, could not be fetched.
- Whether YouTube treats Playables sessions by supervised or age-estimated teen accounts differently (for example, blocked, or ads limited to non-personalized) is not documented in the sources found.

## Q3. Regulation: COPPA, GDPR/GDPR-K, UK Children's Code, EU DSA, EU consumer law, and 2025–2026 addictive-design and streak rules

### Takeaway
Law in force (COPPA as amended, GDPR, UK AADC, DSA, US state laws) mainly regulates *data* and *platforms*. Its pull on engagement design comes through guidance: the ICO's position on nudges and reward loops, and the Commission's DSA guidelines calling for streaks and notifications to be off by default for minors. The rule that would directly reach game streaks and daily-login rewards is the EU "KIDS Act". It was reported as proposed in September 2026 and is **pending, not in force**. The Digital Fairness Act is still expected in Q4 2026. The direction of travel is clear: punitive streaks, re-engagement nudges and false urgency aimed at children are moving from "risky" toward "prohibited" in the EU.

#### COPPA (US) [LAW]
- The amended rule requires **separate verifiable parental consent** for disclosing children's personal information to third parties where that is not integral to the service, for example targeted advertising — [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-coppa-rule-changes-in-the-biden)
- The FTC did **not** adopt the proposed ban on using persistent identifiers under the support-for-internal-operations exception to prompt engagement (for example, push notifications). Operators relying on that exception must now post a notice of the internal operations and how identifiers are limited to them — [Wiley](https://sitepilot12.firmseek.com/client/wiley2/www/printpilot-publication-FTC-Adopts-Amended-Childrens-Online-Protection-Act-Rule.pdf); [Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-amendments-coppa-rule)
- The "directed to children" test now also considers marketing and promotional materials and plans, representations to consumers or third parties, user and third-party reviews, and the age of users on similar services. The rule adds a standalone "mixed audience" definition: services that are child-directed under the multi-factor test but do not target children as their primary audience must age-screen and apply COPPA to users identified as under 13 — [Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2025/05/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule)
- On 25 Feb 2026 the FTC issued a policy statement limiting enforcement against mixed- or general-audience operators that collect children's data solely to verify age without prior parental consent, provided they are otherwise compliant — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)
- Contextual ads are the consent-free route: collecting a persistent identifier solely for internal operations or contextual ads generally does not require parental consent (vendor guide, not legal advice) — [FlexyConsent](https://flexyconsent.com/blog/coppa-childrens-privacy-mobile-games/)
- Enforcement precedent: FTC v. HoYoverse (Genshin Impact), January 2025. The $20M settlement covered loot boxes marketed to children with obscured costs and misleading odds, plus COPPA violations (sharing persistent identifiers with analytics and ad firms). Terms include no loot-box sales to under-16s without parental consent, and disclosure of odds and virtual-currency exchange rates — [FTC business blog](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse); [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/hoyoverse-agrees-to-20-million-settlement-with-ftc-over-loot-boxes)
- FTC staff report "Bringing Dark Patterns to Light" (September 2022) covers gaming among other sectors and flags, for example, apps promoted as "free" that contain in-app purchases — [Arnold & Porter](https://www.arnoldporter.com/en/perspectives/advisories/2022/09/ftc-shines-a-little-light-on-dark-patterns)

#### GDPR / "GDPR-K" (EU) [LAW]
- Art. 8: where consent is the lawful basis for an information society service offered directly to a child, the default age is 16, and member states may lower it to 13. Below that age, consent must come from the holder of parental responsibility, and controllers must make reasonable efforts to verify it — [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/digital-age-of-consent-under-the-gdpr/); [Skillcast](https://www.skillcast.com/blog/gdpr-age-consent-not-childs-play)
- Compiled national ages (sources conflict on Malta, Czechia and possibly Denmark): 13 in Belgium, Denmark, Estonia, Finland, Latvia, Portugal, Sweden and the UK; 14 in Austria, Bulgaria, Cyprus, Italy, Lithuania and Spain; 15 in France, Greece, Slovenia and Czechia; 16 in Germany, Hungary, Ireland, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Poland, Romania and Slovakia — [GDPR Local](https://gdprlocal.com/digital-age-of-consent-under-the-gdpr/); [Skillcast](https://www.skillcast.com/blog/gdpr-age-consent-not-childs-play)

#### UK Age Appropriate Design Code (Children's Code) [statutory code / GUIDANCE]
- Standard 13 (nudge techniques): "Do not use nudge techniques to lead or encourage children to provide unnecessary personal data or turn off privacy protections." The ICO encourages *positive* nudges toward supportive resources and wellbeing tools such as pause and save buttons, and for younger age bands "mid-level pause and save features" — [ICO standard 13](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/childrens-information/childrens-code-guidance-and-resources/age-appropriate-design-a-code-of-practice-for-online-services/13-nudge-techniques/)
- Industry summaries of the Code: providers should not use "reward loops or similar techniques that exploit human susceptibility to reward/pleasure seeking behaviours in order to keep children engaged in their game" (Tiga). ESRB lists reward loops, continuous scrolling, notifications and autoplay as "sticky features" targeted by the Code's detrimental-use provisions (ESRB's standard numbering differs from the ICO's) — [Tiga](https://tiga.org/?p=8283); [ESRB Privacy Certified](https://esrb.org/privacy-certified-blog/the-icos-age-appropriate-design-code-uses-detrimental-to-children)
- ICO "Top tips for games designers" (16 Feb 2023, non-binding best practice): age-appropriate break prompts and checkpoints, helping younger players disengage from long sessions, risk-assessing time-limited or one-time-only offers aimed at children, risk-assessing randomised rewards early, and behavioural profiling for marketing off by default — [Mishcon de Reya](https://mishcon.com/news/ico-publishes-new-top-tips-for-games-designers); [Hill Dickinson](https://www.hilldickinson.com/insights/articles/privacy-protection-children-new-ico-guidance-game-developers)

#### EU Digital Services Act: Art. 28 guidelines on minors [GUIDANCE interpreting LAW]
- Adopted 14 Jul 2025. Not legally binding, but the Commission will use them to assess compliance with Art. 28(1). They apply to online platforms accessible to minors; micro and small enterprises are generally excluded unless designated very large — [European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors); [Merlin/EAO](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10383); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/07/rd-european-commission-guidelines-on-protection-of-minors-under-the-digital-services-act)
- The guidelines call for features that contribute to excessive use, such as communication "streaks", ephemeral content, read receipts, autoplay and push notifications, to be disabled by default, and for removing persuasive design aimed mainly at engagement — [Merlin/EAO](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10383); [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2025/07/15/european-commission-issues-dsa-guidance-on-protecting-children-online-and-release-102kt7n)
- Commentary links autoplay and push notifications, which should be off during core sleep hours, to para 57, and child-friendly time-management tools to para 61; one unverified citation says §61(b) warns against virtual rewards for repeated actions — [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/how-have-platforms-addressed-addictive-design-under-the-dsa/); [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/the-longawaited-eu-guidelines-on-article-281-dsa-what-online-platforms-must-know)
- Commercial practices: transparency about real-money cost and avoidance of intermediate virtual currencies; keep minors away from paid loot boxes and gambling-like features; scarcity, "intermittent or random rewards" and persuasive design are listed as manipulative techniques; minors must not be exposed to advertising that exploits their lack of commercial literacy — [Eurochild briefing](https://eurochild.org/uploads/2025/11/The-DSA-Guidelines-for-the-protection-of-minors-online.pdf); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/european-commission-issues-guidelines-protection-minors); [final annex text](https://www.agcom.it/sites/default/files/media/allegato/2025/EU%20Guidelines_for_minors_online_28%20DSA.pdf)

#### EU consumer protection: CPC Network, Digital Fairness Act and the KIDS Act
- **CPC key principles (21 Mar 2025, non-binding):** seven principles on in-game virtual currencies covering clear real-money pricing, no hidden costs, no forced currency purchases, withdrawal rights, and game design that respects vulnerabilities, especially children's. They were issued alongside action against Star Stable Online, which was cited for direct appeals to children to buy and for time-limited purchase pressure — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658); [Forbrukertilsynet](https://www.forbrukertilsynet.no/eng-articles/national-consumer-authorities-and-commission-take-action-on-harmful-practices-in-the-gaming-industry); [CMS](https://cms.law/en/gbr/legal-updates/level-up-compliance-video-games-industry-in-the-spotlight-again-for-consumer-law-compliance)
- **Digital Fairness Act [PROPOSAL, not yet published]:** a horizontal initiative on dark patterns, addictive design, influencer marketing and unfair personalisation, with attention to minors. Reported possible measures include real-currency equivalents, limits on loot boxes and pay-to-win, limits on addictive design, and clearer standards for "daily streaks" that penalise taking a break. No draft text exists yet — [European Parliament Legislative Train](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-protecting-our-democracy-upholding-our-values/file-digital-fairness-act); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/digital-fairness-act-what-the-public-consultation-tells-the-video-game-industry); [TLT](https://cdn.prod.website-files.com/6809d57c274cc79c237dcb69/6aba80460174513cba85b2f9_TLTD_00350_Digital%20Fairness%20Act%202026_v11.pdf); [CADE](https://cadeproject.org/updates/eu-weighs-stricter-rules-for-addictive-gaming-features/)
- The European Parliament adopted a report in October 2025 calling for a ban on loot boxes in games accessible to minors and for the DFA to address addictive design — [European Parliament press release](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/news/en/press-room/20251013IPR30892/new-eu-measures-needed-to-make-online-services-safer-for-minors)
- The Commission's fitness check reportedly found that at least 31% of EU consumers are affected by addictive design practices (secondary source) — [CADE](https://cadeproject.org/updates/eu-weighs-stricter-rules-for-addictive-gaming-features/)
- **EU KIDS Act [PROPOSAL, around September 2026]:** for children, it would ban autoplay, infinite scroll, "push notifications designed to re-engage users", rewards for sharing or live broadcasting, and "streak mechanics that penalize non-engagement" — [DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal)
- Reported Art. 15 (online games): bars exposing children to features "intended, or can reasonably be foreseen, to encourage compulsive or excessive use". Reportedly this covers penalties or loss of benefits for not playing within a set time, and could reach daily streaks, engagement rewards, re-engagement notifications, and cumulative rewards that require daily logins or vanish after inactivity. A lawyer at Wiggin says it "would prohibit nearly all games… from using many well-known engagement techniques", with "daily log-ins, activity streaks and other time-based mechanisms… under threat" — [PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/daily-log-in-bonuses-and-activity-streaks-under-threat-in-eu-kids-act/); [PocketGamer.biz pocket guide](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/a-pocket-guide-to-the-eu-kids-act-what-games-companies-need-to-know/); [Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/26584/daily-logins-for-rewards-regulated-will-the-eu-kids-act-target-game-design-directly)
- Developers could remove these features for all EU players or age-gate them for adults. Industry groups and Stop Killing Games oppose the proposal — [EarlyGame](https://earlygame.com/news/gaming/the-eus-kids-act-could-kill-games-in-the-name-of-child-safety); [EUobserver](https://euobserver.com/241743/video-game-industry-believes-eu-kids-act-will-drive-small-creators-and-old-games-into-extinction/); [GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/news/5807-draft-of-new-eu-law-proposes-sweeping-restrictions-on-online-games)

#### UK consumer law [LAW]
- Since April 2025 the DMCC Act lets the CMA fine up to £300,000 or 10% of global turnover, whichever is higher, without going to court. In scope are harmful online choice architecture ("dark patterns"), false urgency and aggressive practices that exploit vulnerability. The CMA has already acted against misleading countdown clocks (Wowcher, Simba Sleep) — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/new-uk-consumer-rules-herald-stricter-enforcement-and-significant-fines); [Birketts](https://www.birketts.co.uk/legal-update/the-new-consumer-enforcement-landscape-one-year-in/)

#### US state "addictive design" laws [LAW]
- **California SB 976:** parental consent is required before minors receive "addictive feeds". Notifications to minors are barred 12am–6am and, Monday to Friday from September to May, 8am–3pm, without verifiable parental consent. The default time limit is one hour a day — [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/uncategorized/california-passes-law-to-protect-minors-from-addictive-feeds/); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/11/california-ag-seeks-comment-on-social-media-addiction-law)
- On 9 Sep 2025 the Ninth Circuit largely upheld SB 976 at the preliminary-injunction stage (addictive-feed and private-default provisions) but found the like-count provision likely unconstitutional. Wikipedia's infobox conflicts with this — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/ninth-circuit-upholds-addictive-social-media-feed-ban-and-default-privacy-settings-for-minors-in-californias-protecting-our-kids-from-social-media-addiction-act); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protecting_Our_Kids_from_Social_Media_Addiction_Act)

It restricts addictive features for under-18s — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/new-york-attorney-general-releases-final-rules-for-safe-for-kids-act); [Inside Privacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/childrens-privacy/new-york-publishes-final-safe-for-kids-act-rules/)
- **Vermont AADC:** applies to services "reasonably likely to be accessed" by minors and limits notifications to minors, including late at night. **Nebraska AADC:** actual-knowledge standard, with an exception where fewer than 2% of users are minors; some default-setting duties apply only to social media. Neither had been challenged in court at the time of reporting — [FPF](https://fpf.org/blog/vermont-and-nebraska-diverging-experiments-in-state-age-appropriate-design-codes/); [WSGR](https://www.wsgrdataadvisor.com/2025/06/nebraska-and-vermont-pass-age-appropriate-design-codes/)

### Inferences
- **Who is the regulated party?** On Playables, YouTube is the platform (a DSA very large online platform and a COPPA operator for its own data collection). A single-player game developer that collects no personal data of its own (progress saved through the platform, ads served by YouTube) has much less direct COPPA and GDPR exposure. That changes if the game adds its own analytics or identifiers. The DSA Art. 28 guidelines bind platforms, but YouTube can push them downstream through Playables policy. The KIDS Act's reported Art. 15 would target *online games* directly, as would the DFA.
- **Daily streaks:** under current law they are not prohibited for this game. They are explicitly disfavoured for minors in DSA guidance (default off) and ICO-adjacent guidance (reward loops), and directly targeted by the pending KIDS Act if they "penalise non-engagement". A *non-punitive* streak (no loss of earned items, grace days or "freezes", celebrating total days played rather than consecutive days) is the defensible form.
- **Notifications:** the game has none, which avoids the CA, VT, DSA and KIDS Act notification rules entirely. Off-platform re-engagement prompts should not be added.
- **US state social-media laws** (SB 976, SAFE for Kids) target feeds and social platforms and are unlikely to apply to a puzzle game. They do signal where US standards on notifications and time limits are heading.
- **COPPA risk vector:** the new "directed to children" factors (marketing materials, reviews, the audience of similar services) mean that how the game is promoted can make it child-directed even if the design does not change.

### Gaps
- Exact final paragraph numbers and wording of the DSA guidelines on streaks and rewards (EUR-Lex text not fetched).
- EU KIDS Act: formal title, adoption date, final Art. 15 text, transition period and any small-developer exemptions are not confirmed.
- No research was done on whether the UK Online Safety Act or Ofcom's children's codes reach single-player games without user interaction. They likely do not, since they target user-to-user and search services, but this was not verified.
- UK Children's Code commencement date (background: conformance expected from 2 September 2021) was not re-verified this session.
- The COPPA civil penalty figure ($53,088 per violation, per a compliance blog) was not verified against an FTC source.

## Q4. Ad standards: rules on ads to children (CARU, CAP), rewarded-ad disclosure, and bans on accidental clicks and deceptive placement

### Takeaway
Across CARU, the CAP Code, Google Families and AdMob the standards agree: ads must be *obviously identifiable as ads* to a child. The classic violation is an unlabeled treasure chest or "next level" button that opens an ad. Exit controls must be clear. Rewarded ads must be opt-in with the reward disclosed first. Ads must not interrupt play or be placed to cause accidental taps. For a textless game, the "obviously identifiable" test has to be passed with icons and visual conventions alone.

### Cited Findings
**CARU (US, under-13s) [SELF-REG, referenced by regulators]**
- Revised guidelines took effect in January 2022 and cover children under 13 on all platforms, with a dedicated in-app and in-game section — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/social-media/1102110/caru-releases-revised-childrens-advertising-guidelines); [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/caru-announces-changes-to-guidelines-for-2022.md)
- Blurring guideline: ads woven into a game must be easily identifiable as ads. A treasure chest that opens an ad before granting in-game rewards must be properly disclosed, and without clear disclosure it would likely fail — [BBB National Programs (CARU) for developers](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/bd/insights/2021/09/14/developers-and-caru-advertising-guidelines)
- Ads, apps and games must not use "unfair, deceptive, or other manipulative tactics, including deceptive door openers, social pressure, or validation" to get children to view ads or buy, or cause accidental engagement. Ads must not be so small, or disguised as other features, that children tap them by accident. Position, colour or size must not be used to manipulate. Dismiss and exit controls must be "clear and conspicuous" to children, and purchases must make clear that real money is involved — [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/bd/insights/2021/09/14/developers-and-caru-advertising-guidelines); [BBB: In-app advertising and kids](https://bbbprograms.org/media/insights/blog/in-app-ads-kids); [CARU guidelines PDF](https://bbbnp-bbbp-stf-use1-01.s3.amazonaws.com/docs/default-source/caru/caru_advertisingguidelines.pdf)
- Cases: CARU brought four cases against children's apps involving blurring, including ad buttons designed to look like they led to the next stage of the game — [Lexology (FTC stealth advertising event)](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e18111e8-4334-48a5-b777-c4e2ea935233). Tilting Point Media: ads "unduly interfered with gameplay" and "encouraged excessive ad viewing by children through deceptive door openers"; the fix included an exit option on the first ad screen — [PR Newswire (CARU)](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/caru-finds-tilting-point-media-in-violation-of-coppa-and-carus-advertising-and-privacy-guidelines-company-agrees-to-corrective-actions-301617562.html). Outfit7: in-app ads blurred the line between ads and game content — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=d88a3bea-9304-437e-9649-55fc2c9a8a59)

**UK CAP Code / ASA [SELF-REG, co-regulatory backstop]**
- Section 5 governs ads aimed at or featuring children, defined as under 16. Advergames, including in apps, must be "obviously identifiable" as marketing, and most ASA advergame complaints concern children's games — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/new-cap-helpnote-on-advergames/); [SCL](https://www.scl.org/2758-advergames-play-with-caution/)
- Language that pressures children into in-game purchases likely breaches the CAP Code and possibly UK consumer law — [iGaming Business](https://igamingbusiness.com/marketing-affiliates/advertising-and-children-the-particular-appeal-of-proper-guidance/)
- ASA ruling, February 2023 (Fuero Games, mobile puzzle game ads): ads depicting injured children breached the Code, and the advertiser had shown no steps to target the ads away from under-18s — [Osborne Clarke](https://marketinglaw.osborneclarke.com/advertising-regulation/asa-issues-warning-on-the-depiction-of-physical-injury-in-in-game-ads/)

**Rewarded-ad disclosure and placement (Google benchmarks) [PLATFORM]**
- AdMob: disclose the required action and the reward before *each* rewarded ad; disclose bundles; serve only after an affirmative, unambiguous opt-in; never oblige interaction — [AdMob rewarded policy](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578). A developer report says each rewarded ad must be individually opted into, with no "auto-play rewarded ads" toggle (anecdotal) — [Godot Q&A](https://ask.godotengine.org/104083/admob-policy-violation)
- Families: no interstitial at launch; no ad that interferes with play and is not closeable within 5 seconds; no deceptive or inadvertent-click design; no dismiss button that triggers another ad — [Families preview](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/17122218); [Google Play Families](https://goo.gle/families)
- General Play policy: no full-screen interstitial unclosable after 15 seconds; no ads mimicking app UI or system notifications — [Play Ads policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753)

### Inferences
- In a textless game, the "watch an ad for a reward" control needs a universal, distinct ad marker (for example a TV or play-triangle glyph, visually different from normal game buttons) plus a picture of the reward. It must never share the shape or colour of "next level" or "play" buttons. This is exactly the CARU and Outfit7 blurring pattern.
- Interstitials belong only at natural breaks (after a level-complete screen), never on launch, mid-level, or immediately after a tap that a child expects to advance the game. Frequency caps and a first-session grace period reduce the "excessive ad viewing" risk CARU found at Tilting Point.
- Declining a rewarded ad must carry no penalty and no guilt-tripping (no sad animal, no "are you sure?" loop). CARU treats "social pressure" and "deceptive door openers" as manipulative.

### Gaps
- No CARU decisions from 2024–2026 specific to rewarded ads were found. Whether CARU updated its guidelines after 2022 was not confirmed.
- No Playables-specific disclosure rule for rewarded ads was found; the AdMob and Families rules are used as benchmarks.
- No research was done on how the ASA or CAP treat rewarded ads specifically, as opposed to advergames.

## Q5. Ethics and dark patterns: which engagement techniques are considered manipulative, especially for children, and evidence of backlash

### Takeaway
Researchers, NGOs and regulators consistently treat the following as manipulative when aimed at children: loss-framed streaks, FOMO timers and false urgency, intermittent or random (variable-ratio) rewards, nagging, and characters that guilt children into continuing. Empirical harm evidence for streaks specifically is thin, and one major ed-tech platform (Khan Academy) retired its streaks anyway. Players clearly prefer opt-in rewarded ads to forced interstitials.

### Cited Findings
- Radesky et al., *JAMA Network Open* 2022: 160 children aged 3–5, coding the apps each child used most. About 4 in 5 apps used manipulative design, more often in apps used by children of less-educated parents. Examples: pop-ups urging a return to the game, purchase prompts framed as "ask your parents", and characters scolding idle children ("Come back tomorrow and get a dragon") — [PMC full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/); [Michigan Medicine](https://www.michiganmedicine.org/health-lab/design-tricks-commonly-used-monetize-young-childrens-app-use). A later testimony describes 133 apps, which conflicts with the 160-children framing — [Vermont legislature testimony](https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2024/WorkGroups/Senate%20Economic%20Development/Bills/S.289/Witness%20Documents/S.289~Jenny%20Radesky~Kids%20Code%20Testimony~3-1-2024.pdf)
- 5Rights Foundation, *Disrupted Childhood* (2018; updated version 2, April 2023): reward loops, notifications, streaks, likes and autoplay form an engagement ecosystem with costs to children's mental health, sleep, education and autonomy. Responsibility lies with companies — [5Rights 2023](https://5rightsfoundation.com/in-action/disrupted-childhood-the-cost-of-persuasive-design-2023.html); [5Rights: Persuasive design](https://5rightsfoundation.com/our-work/design-of-service/persuasive-design.html). Commentary on the report says it proposes default "streak holidays" and built-in timeouts — [New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/?p=109401)
- In 2024, before the DSA guidelines were final, civil-society groups asked the Commission to rule out intermittent reward systems, gambling-like features and endless scroll — [5Rights coalition note](https://5rightsfoundation.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/241001_Note-on-the-EC-Art.-28-DSA-guidelines-protection-of-minors_SIGNATURES.pdf)
- Regulatory characterisation, in brief: the DSA guidelines name scarcity, intermittent or random rewards and persuasive design as manipulative for minors — [Eurochild](https://eurochild.org/uploads/2025/11/The-DSA-Guidelines-for-the-protection-of-minors-online.pdf). The CPC flagged time-limited purchase pressure and direct appeals to children — [Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658). The ICO tips say to risk-assess time-limited offers and randomised rewards — [Mishcon de Reya](https://mishcon.com/news/ico-publishes-new-top-tips-for-games-designers). CARU names "social pressure" and "deceptive door openers" — [BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/bd/insights/2021/09/14/developers-and-caru-advertising-guidelines). The CMA targets false urgency and countdown clocks — [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/new-uk-consumer-rules-herald-stricter-enforcement-and-significant-fines)
- Streak evidence: a Northwestern IPR working paper (2026) notes "limited causal evidence" on streaks, studies highlighting streaks for grades 4–6 in Peru, and reports that Khan Academy retired its streak feature over worries it could harm motivation in some circumstances — [Northwestern IPR WP 26-05](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf); [J-PAL](https://povertyactionlab.org/media/file-research-paper/streaking-success-effects-highlighting-streaks-student-effort-and)
- van Essen et al. (2023), as summarised: Snapchat streaks are associated with problematic smartphone use, but with a small effect size. The original paper was not verified — [UBC ETEC blog](https://blogs.ubc.ca/etec523/?p=11212)
- Player preference: a 2020 Tapjoy study found 76% of US mobile gamers prefer opt-in rewarded ads vs 24% preferring mandatory ads — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-consumers-appreciate-in-game-ads); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/302685/mobile-gamers-prefer-rewarded-video-ads.html). A Unity survey found 46% prefer incentivized video vs 29% full-screen pop-ups, 20% banners and 5% standard interstitial video — [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/unity-the-majority-of-mobile-gamers-are-open-to-engaging-with-incentivized-video-ads/). These are ad-tech vendor surveys.
- Backlash examples: creator protests over YouTube's MFK change ("COPPAcalypse", "Dead Upload Day") — [Reclaim The Net](https://reclaimthenet.org/dead-upload-day-youtube-made-for-kids-coppa). Games-industry and Stop Killing Games opposition to the EU KIDS Act, the regulatory direction going the other way — [EUobserver](https://euobserver.com/241743/video-game-industry-believes-eu-kids-act-will-drive-small-creators-and-old-games-into-extinction/)

### Inferences
- Ranked by how manipulative they are considered for children, most to least, based on how often they are named across the sources above:
  1. disguised or "blurred" ads and characters guilting or scolding the player;
  2. loss-framed streaks, especially paired with "watch an ad to save your streak";
  3. countdown timers and limited-time offers;
  4. random or variable rewards;
  5. nagging re-engagement prompts.
  
  Deterministic rewards (stars, collection slots, cosmetic hats earned by progress) are not criticised in any source found.
- Pairing loss aversion with an ad ("your streak will break unless you watch") combines two flagged mechanisms: loss-framed streaks and pressure to view ads (CARU's "deceptive door openers"). For a mixed audience that includes young children, it is the single highest-risk pattern.
- The evidence for streak *harm* is weak, but the regulatory trend (DSA guidelines, KIDS Act proposal, 5Rights) means the auditor should judge streaks on their framing (punitive vs celebratory) rather than on proven harm.

### Gaps
- No rigorous study isolates harm from streaks, or from variable rewards, in children's casual games.
- No independent (non-vendor) data was found on interstitial ads driving uninstalls or churn.
- The Epic Games FTC dark-patterns settlement (2022) amount and details were not verified this session.

## Q6. Practical implications: which monetization levers stay safe for a mixed child/adult audience, and how eCPMs change for child-directed traffic

### Takeaway
Child-directed or under-18 traffic gets non-personalized or contextual ads only and earns several times less. Estimates range from 2.5× to over 5× lower eCPM, and early YouTube MFK estimates put revenue drops at 60–90%. Mislabelling a general-audience game as child-directed is therefore expensive, and so is designing it in a way that draws mostly children. The levers that remain safe are those that respect choice: opt-in, clearly marked rewarded ads; well-spaced interstitials at natural breaks; and deterministic, non-punitive progression rewards.

### Cited Findings
- Unity: an app designated child-directed may serve only contextual, non-targeted ads to *all* users; when the age signal is unspecified, contextual is the default — [Unity Ads docs](https://docs.unity.com/ads/en/COPPACompliance.html)
- Ad-monetization consultancy, data from three of its own games: COPPA-user eCPMs run 2.5× to more than 5× lower than non-COPPA traffic, and marking an app child-directed "could be costing you 70% of your eCPM" (single-operator, anecdotal) — [GameBiz Consulting](https://www.gamebizconsulting.com/newsletter/admon-newsletter-11-the-shrinking-island-ad-monetization-underage-users)
- Contextual eCPMs are typically lower than personalized ones; publishers recover value through direct family-friendly deals (vendor guide; no benchmark given) — [FlexyConsent](https://flexyconsent.com/blog/coppa-childrens-privacy-mobile-games/)
- Gaming publishers commonly treat traffic that cannot be age-verified as contextual-only by default and segment ad configurations by age tier (vendor blog, August 2026) — [Publir](https://publir.com/blog/2026/08/the-coppa-yield-trap-how-gaming-publishers-segment-traffic-t/)
- Public mobile-game eCPM benchmarks explicitly exclude kids' apps, so general benchmarks do not transfer to child traffic — [Tenjin](https://tenjin.com/blog/ad-mon-gaming-2026/)
- YouTube MFK: creators feared revenue drops of 60–90%. An initial creator test found 60–90% lower revenue without personalized ads, with anecdotal reports of about 80% declines. An FTC-hosted economic study examines the settlement's effects — [FTC-hosted paper, "COPPAcalypse" (Garrett A. ### Inferences
**Lever classification for the audit.** These are inferences, built from the platform, regulatory and guidance findings in Q2–Q5.

| Lever | Classification | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Opt-in rewarded ads, one tap per ad, reward shown as a picture before the ad, reward always granted, no penalty for declining | **Allowed / safest** | AdMob opt-in and disclosure rules; CARU blurring; player preference data |
| Rewarded-ad entry point that looks like a normal game button (chest or "next" arrow with no ad marker) | **Off-limits** | CARU blurring cases (treasure chest; "next stage" lookalike buttons); Families "inadvertent clicks" |
| Interstitials at natural breaks (after level-complete), capped, with a no-ads grace period early in a session | **Allowed** | Families/Play placement rules; CARU "unduly interfered with gameplay" (Tilting Point) |
| Interstitial at launch or resume, mid-level, or right after a tap expected to advance the game | **Off-limits** | Families no-launch rule; CARU and Families accidental-click rules |
| High interstitial frequency (for example, after every level) | **Risky** | CARU "excessive ad viewing" finding; 13+ audience and child spill-over |
| Daily streak with loss on a missed day | **Risky now; likely restricted in the EU for children if the KIDS Act passes** | DSA guidelines (streaks off by default for minors); KIDS Act proposal ("streak mechanics that penalize non-engagement"); 5Rights; ICO-adjacent reward-loop guidance |
| Non-punitive streak (grace days or freeze, keep all earned items, celebrate total days) | **Allowed / low risk** | Matches 5Rights "streak holidays" and ICO positive-nudge guidance; avoids "loss of benefits" framing |
| "Watch an ad to save your streak" or "to avoid losing progress" | **Off-limits for a mixed child audience** | Combines loss aversion with ad pressure: CARU door openers and social pressure; KIDS Act loss-of-benefit framing |
| "Watch an ad to continue / get an extra move" after a failed level | **Risky; acceptable if fully optional and the level can simply be retried for free** | CARU manipulative tactics; DSA commercial-literacy guidance |
| Countdown timers, limited-time hats, FOMO events | **Risky to off-limits** | CPC time-limited pressure; ICO tips risk-assess time-limited offers; CMA false urgency |
| Random or variable reward chests (even if free) | **Risky** | DSA guidelines list "intermittent or random rewards"; ICO tips risk-assess randomised rewards; free chests are not loot boxes under the paid-loot-box rules |
| Deterministic star ratings, collections, cosmetic hats earned through play | **Allowed** | No source criticises deterministic progress rewards; no real-money link |
| Characters expressing sadness or guilt when the player leaves or declines an ad | **Off-limits** | Radesky et al. (scolding characters); CARU social pressure |
| Re-engagement push notifications or off-platform nags | **Off-limits** (and not present) | DSA default-off; KIDS Act; CA and VT notification limits |
| Break and stop cues (natural end-of-session points, "good place to stop") | **Recommended** | ICO pause/save and break prompts; DSA time-management tools |
| Own analytics or tracking SDKs collecting identifiers | **Avoid** | COPPA 2025 third-party disclosure consent; HoYoverse identifier-sharing count |
| Marketing the game as "for kids" or for toddlers | **Off-limits** | Playables MUST NOT be made for kids; COPPA marketing-materials factor |

- **Revenue logic:** compliance and revenue point the same way. Adult-appealing "cozy" positioning keeps the game eligible under Playables' 13+ rule and lifts the share of personalized-ad-eligible adult traffic. Child-heavy traffic will be served non-personalized ads by YouTube anyway, at a fraction of the eCPM. Aggressive ad levers therefore earn little from the very users they most put at risk.
- **Developer control:** on Playables the developer cannot segment ad treatment by age, because YouTube decides. The developer's levers are ad *timing, frequency, framing and disclosure*, and the game's overall positioning.

### Gaps
- No public, independent eCPM benchmark for kids' or child-directed traffic. SuperAwesome, Kidoz and PubMatic figures were not found in search results.
- No public data on Playables eCPMs, or on how YouTube's under-18 or supervised handling affects Playables ad revenue specifically.
- No measured post-2020 average for the MFK revenue drop; the 60–90% range comes from early tests and creator anecdotes.
