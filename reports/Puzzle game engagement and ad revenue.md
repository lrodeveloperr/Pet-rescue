# Fair puzzles retain, opt-in rewards pay

Pet Rescue: Borrow & Escape maximizes engagement by making each generated puzzle fair, easy on the critical path and quick to finish, and by making the return trip forgiving; it maximizes ad revenue mainly by adding opt-in rewarded placements at moments of need, not by showing more interstitials. The strongest evidence (A/B tests on more than 45,000 and 27,000 players, a 20,000-session difficulty experiment and seven experiments on broken streaks) says casual players engage most when assigned difficulty is easy, gain little from explicit tutorials when verbs can be discovered, play less when optional objectives feel mandatory, and drop off after a broken streak unless they can repair it. Ad evidence is mostly vendor-published but points one way: adding interstitials to one mobile game bought about 18% more ARPDAU (ad revenue per daily active user) for a 2.3–2.5% relative D7 loss, interstitials churn players more than rewarded ads, and rewarded ads perform best when the player needs help. Pet Rescue's interstitial rules (3-level grace, at most one per 3 levels and per 3 minutes, a 90-second cooldown after a rewarded ad) already equal the strictest web-portal cap, so its revenue upside sits in a thin rewarded menu, not in ad frequency. Three hard limits bound every lever: YouTube Playables requires a 13+ general-audience game that is not "made for kids", forbids every external network call including the developer's own analytics, and serves ads only through a preview SDK whose revenue share is a closed pilot with no published terms. Children will play a cute, textless, one-finger animal game regardless, which turns "watch an ad to save your streak", random hats for ad views and countdown offers into liabilities under the US children's-advertising guidelines (CARU), the EU's Digital Services Act (DSA) guidelines on minors and the pending EU KIDS Act. Most figures here come from search-engine summaries rather than full documents, and none has been validated on Playables, which publishes no retention, eCPM or fill benchmarks and leaves the game no way to run its own A/B tests.

## Four evidence grades separate binding rules from vendor folklore

Every principle below carries a grade. It tells an auditor whether a miss is a certification failure, a likely engagement loss or only a departure from industry folklore. The grades describe the quality of the evidence, not how confident this report is in the advice. One caveat caps every grade. The research behind this report could not open most primary pages, because the environment's proxy blocked developers.google.com, support.google.com, arXiv and most trade press. Every finding therefore comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages. Figures marked "secondary" were reported by a page citing the original. Check the official Playables wording against the live documentation before any pass/fail verdict depends on it.

| Grade | What qualifies | How an audit should treat it |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule** | A Playables certification MUST / MUST NOT, or a law in force (labelled "Law" where the source is legislation) | Pass/fail. A miss blocks publishing or creates legal exposure |
| **Rule-S** | A Playables SHOULD / SHOULD NOT | Expected. A deviation needs a written reason |
| **Strong** | Controlled experiments, peer-reviewed studies, or large datasets with a disclosed method | Comply unless the game's own data contradicts it |
| **Moderate** | Company-published test results with partial method (Duolingo, King, Unity/ironSource), analyst teardowns of observable data, regulator guidance, self-regulatory codes | Default practice. Test before deviating |
| **Weak** | Vendor blogs, unsourced benchmarks, self-reported earnings, single anecdotes, opinion pieces, unofficial Playables guides | A hypothesis only, never a pass/fail criterion |
| **Pending** | Proposed legislation | Direction of travel. Prefer designs that would survive it |
| **Inference** | A threshold this report derives from the evidence | A starting value to test, not a finding |

## YouTube's certification rules fix the frame before design begins

Playables is a reviewed storefront, and a game that fails any MUST requirement "will not be allowed to publish" ([Playables certification requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements)). The rules that affect engagement concentrate on the first seconds and on interruptions. The initial bundle **must be under 30 MiB and should be under 15 MiB**, and the game should be **interactive in under 5 seconds**, measured from page load to the `gameReady` call ([Stability & performance requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_stability)); FRVR's channel documentation adds that calling `gameReady` while the game is still loading is grounds for rejection ([FRVR docs](https://docs.frvr.com/requirements/channels/youtube/)). The game must stop on `onPause`, resume on `onResume`, play no sound while YouTube is muted, and should not offer an overall mute button ([Integration requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_integration)). Progress must go through `saveData` at meaningful milestones such as a level change, only after `loadData` resolves, and stay under 3 MiB; as the requirement is reported, no other save mechanism is allowed, and signed-out players simply receive empty data ([Integration requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_integration); [SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/sdk)). The rule with the widest reach is that Playables **"must not make any external calls," and internal analytics are not exempt** ([Certification FAQ](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/support/certification_faq)).

Pet Rescue's ~200 KB build is about 150 times under the 30 MiB limit, so its certification risk lies elsewhere. The likely failures are a three.js or font file fetched from a CDN, an in-game mute toggle, a `gameReady` call that fires before level 1 accepts a tap, or a localStorage save fallback.

Discovery is browse-driven. Players arrive through the Playables tab in the Explore menu, a shelf on the Home feed, direct `youtube.com/playables/<id>` links and, since September 2025, creators' livestreams ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2024/05/28/youtube-playables-rollout/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/16/youtube-live-gets-a-major-update-introduces-dual-format-streaming-minigames-and-more/)). One catalogue site reports that the section has no search box (weak) ([playables.games](https://playables.games/how-to-play/)). A **pre-roll plays automatically while the game loads** ([Monetization reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/monetization)), so a new player sees an ad first, then the load, then the first tap. Thumbnails, titles and descriptions must contain **no branding or logos** ([Design requirements](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_design)). Accessibility tags became a discovery input on 24 August 2026, and duplicate builds were banned on 25 August 2026 ([Revision history](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/revisionhistory)).

The niche is proven but crowded. Tracker data counts plays rather than users. It puts the Brain & Puzzle genre at 611 games and 1.1B plays, with **Save The Pets at about 37M plays and Cat Escape at about 24M** ([playables.games](https://playables.games/genres/brain-puzzle/)). A publisher's analysis ranked Dog Escape among the top new games of summer 2026 at 274K plays a day, while the catalogue grew from 788 to 1,477 titles in about two months ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-game-mechanics)). A textless one-finger rescue puzzle fits the shape that wins: short levels you understand at a glance. It still has to stand out in its thumbnail and its first level.

The money side is immature. Ads run only through YouTube's SDK, whose `ads` namespace is a **"public preview API subject to change without notice"** ([SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/sdk)). In-app purchases (IAP), banners and off-platform monetization are unavailable. A Google partnerships lead said the company aims to "test IAP solutions during 2027" ([Mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/youtube-has-quietly-rolled-out-playables-across-europe/)). Google has published no revenue-share rate, eCPM or payout schedule ([shattered.io](https://shattered.io/youtube-playables-50-markets-zero-iap-2026/)). The publisher Playgama says ad monetization began as a limited test in **March 2026**, that its games averaged **about $3,100 a month** in that closed beta, and that its top titles made $10,000–15,000 in their first month. This is self-reported sales material, graded weak ([shattered.io](https://shattered.io/youtube-playables-50-markets-zero-iap-2026/); [Playgama](https://playgama.com/publish-your-game-on-youtube-playables/)). Google itself says monetization may not yet be available to every developer account, and that the portal's ad settings "won't change the end-user ads experience for now" ([YouTube Playables](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables); [Developer Portal](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/developer_portal)). A third-party source reports that YouTube Premium subscribers see no Playables ads (unverified) ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-monetization)). Playables reached 50+ markets, including the EU and the Nordics, by late September 2026 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/youtube-playables-expands-to-more-than-50-markets-including-the-eu-and-nordics/)), although other sources date the EU rollout to July.

The ban on external calls has a consequence the ad playbook ignores: **Pet Rescue cannot run its own A/B tests on Playables.** It cannot send telemetry, and the duplicate-build ban rules out listing parallel variants. The portal's own analytics showed only total plays until mid-July 2026, when age, country and session-length breakdowns arrived ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-updates)). Third-party guides say per-game revenue arrives as a monthly breakdown around the 15th (unverified) ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-updates)). Every threshold this report grades Inference or Weak must therefore be tested on web portals that allow telemetry, or read roughly by comparing successive Playables releases, before it becomes a standard.

## The puzzle carries retention, so every generated level must earn its place

Analysts of the strongest puzzle franchises agree that the core puzzle, not the meta layer, carries retention. Deconstructor of Fun calls Royal Match's appeal "mainly the puzzle". Naavik observes that players "care less and less" about meta features the closer a game is to classic puzzle play ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2021/3/21/royal-match-the-new-king-from-turkey); [Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/why-dream-games-success-is-a-challenge-to-replicate/)). For a game of 300 solver-generated levels of 12–39 moves each, level curation is therefore the highest-leverage engagement work.

### Difficulty should rise and fall in a sawtooth and stay easy on the critical path

Successful puzzle games ramp up in cycles. A CHI PLAY 2014 analysis of Portal, Braid and Lemmings found that each game introduces one skill at a time in simple puzzles, then practises it, then combines it with earlier skills, with difficulty rising until the next skill arrives ([Linehan et al.](https://cora.ucc.ie/handle/10468/3461)). A level generator built to produce a **sawtooth difficulty curve was validated on playtraces from almost 1,000 players**. Its authors credit the sudden drops after hard runs with keeping players in flow ([arXiv 1905.06379](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06379)). One analyst's level-by-level reading of Royal Match's first 100 levels shows the commercial version of the pattern. Normal levels average **about 1.2 attempts, hard levels about 1.6 and super-hard peaks up to about 2.5**. Each new mechanic gets 2–3 learning boards, and an instant-win bonus level follows every tenth level ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)). King draws two rules from more than 15,000 Candy Crush levels. First, **"if a level is really hard, it should also be really short."** Second, a level's fun is scored by comparing time-to-abandon with time-to-pass, and the least-fun levels are constantly pruned ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/); [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1034392/Sugary-Statistics-What-We-Have)).

The evidence on how hard the critical path should be is clearer than designers often assume. Across more than 20,000 sessions, Lomas et al. found that **when difficulty was assigned, the easiest variants motivated players most**; moderate difficulty won only when players chose it themselves ([CMU HCII](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games)). A pre-registered study of 311 players found no significant effect of the difficulty-to-skill ratio on enjoyment at all ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9890114/)), and an EA system that treated churn as a state to avoid raised engagement by up to 9% ([Xue et al.](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/322414816_Dynamic_Difficulty_Adjustment_for_Maximized_Engagement_in_Digital_Games)). King's data science director puts the trade-off plainly: easing a level costs conversions but "saves players from churning" ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/)).

Playables players never chose Pet Rescue's difficulty. The critical path should therefore lean easy, and hard challenge should live in optional par stars. Because Pet Rescue has no fail state, restarts, hint uses and time-to-solve stand in for attempts. Monument Valley shows what is at stake. Even in that short, gentle game, Android achievement rates fall from about 96% for early chapters to **about 17–19% for later ones** ([Exophase](https://www.exophase.com/game/monument-valley-android/achievements/)). Pet Rescue's best mechanics belong in the first third of its 300 levels, not the last.

### Each level needs one forced idea, proven by turning features off in the solver

Respected designers build each level around one required insight and then delete the boring alternatives. Alan Hazelden starts from "a single interaction, or chain of interactions, that I want you to have to do" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/the-relaxing-open-world-puzzle-design-of-i-a-monster-s-expedition-i-)). Arvi Teikari says "a very large part" of Baba Is You's testing went into "eliminating alternative solutions that aren't interesting enough" ([Indie Game Website](https://www.indiegamewebsite.com/2019/07/19/baba-is-you-developer-arvi-teikari-talks-indie-innovation-influences-and-puzzle-design/)). Game Maker's Toolkit's favoured device is the "catch", in which the obvious path turns out to fail ([ResetEra](https://www.resetera.com/threads/what-makes-a-good-puzzle-game-makers-toolkit.29537/)).

Sokoban research explains why generated levels can feel like bookkeeping rather than insight. Perceived difficulty tracks a level's structure. In a user study on computer-generated levels, **box count correlated with perceived difficulty at r ≈ 0.48 and congestion at r ≈ 0.43** ([Kartal et al.](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIIDE/article/download/12859/12706/16375)). Solution length, by contrast, correlated with human solve time at only about 0.47 (a secondary figure). In the same human dataset, **73.8% of game configurations were dead (unsolvable), but players spent only about 14.5% of their time in them** ([Jarušek & Pelánek](http://sokoban.dk/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/Difficulty-Rating-of-Sokoban-Puzzle.pdf)). In Lily's Garden, the move counts from the best runs of a *weaker* AI agent predicted real players' completion rates better than the strongest agent did, because the strongest agent appeared to memorise levels ([arXiv 2306.14626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.14626)).

For Pet Rescue this means three solver checks per level: the level becomes unsolvable when the companion or hazard it features is disabled, a greedy route straight to the goal fails, and only a few distinct solutions come close to par. It also means estimating human difficulty with a deliberately limited agent rather than the optimal solver, since in Lily's Garden the strongest agent's play was the worst guide to how humans fared, and a generator tuned only for "solvable and hard" can manufacture difficulty through tedium (long routes, many boulders) instead of insight.

### Forced levels teach better than tutorials when the verbs can be discovered

Text-free teaching is backed by the best tutorial experiment in games research. Andersen et al. tested 8 tutorial designs in 3 games with **more than 45,000 players. Tutorials raised play time by up to 29%, but only in the most complex game**, and gave no significant gain in the two simpler ones. The authors question tutorial investment "for games with mechanics that can be discovered through experimentation" ([Andersen et al., CHI 2012](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/chi2012/chi2012.pdf)). A 2020 study found that tutorials still help non-expert players' flow ([MTI](https://doi.org/10.3390/mti4030041)). For a textless game, the answer is the forced level. The first level with each companion should allow only one route, which uses the new verb once. Two or three variations should follow before any difficulty spike, as Royal Match does with its learning boards ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)). Hybrid-casual hits put players into a level with "no tutorials or extra taps" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/2/3/hybridcasual-puzzles-expanding-the-puzzle-market)).

No credible benchmark exists for how fast the first win should come. The often-quoted claim that D1 is "largely decided in the first 90 seconds" is unsourced vendor copy ([VMobify](https://vmobify.com/blog/app-retention-strategy)). A Poki engineer's warning that each extra megabyte loses "a couple percent of players" is a practitioner's remark, not a study ([Poki](https://poki.com/blog/building-web-browser-games-2026)). A first rescue within 30–60 seconds of load is this report's inference, to be measured rather than assumed.

### Celebrate success without hiding the board

Controlled studies agree that "juice" (extra animation, sound and haptic feedback) improves how a game looks and feels, and disagree on whether it helps people play. Visual embellishments improved visual appeal in every game tested but affected players' sense of competence only in specific circumstances ([Hicks et al., CHI PLAY 2019](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Juicy-Game-Design:-Understanding-the-Impact-of-on-Hicks-Gerling/5914c05b99f717e4ada667e1b23630493eabf3ad)), and haptic embellishments improved enjoyment, aesthetic appeal and immersion ([Waterloo, CHI 2021](https://uwaterloo.ca/haptic-experience-lab/projects/juicy-haptic-design-vibrotactile-embellishments-can-improve)). Kao et al. found that **exaggerated feedback lowered players' self-rated competence**, while **curiosity was the strongest predictor of enjoyment and playtime** ([Kao et al., CHI 2024](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656)), and Juul and Begy's players rated the juicy version of a game higher yet performed worse in it ([Juul & Begy](https://adk.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/good-feedback-for-bad-players-a-preliminary-study-of-juicy-interf)).

No public study links juice to retention in mobile puzzle games. The auditable consequences are threefold. Big celebrations should fire only on real accomplishments: a rescue, a companion's first use, an under-par finish. Ice slides, currents, bees and crumbling bridges should animate readably and be skippable. And the level-complete screen should tease the next companion, biome or hat, because curiosity outperforms particle effects.

### Free undo, a stepped hint and an exit route prevent rage-quits

Unlimited, instant undo is the norm in respected Sokoban-likes because it makes puzzles "less tedious", not easier. That rests on community and designer consensus rather than data ([Steam Puzzle Lovers](https://steamcommunity.com/groups/puzzlelovers/discussions/0/7340374196882201829/)). Hint research is firmer. Surveys found that most puzzle hint systems are rule-based and badly timed against players' actual progress. An adaptive, progressive hint system **significantly improved player experience** in a controlled experiment ([Hao et al., 2022](https://faculty.washington.edu/weicaics/paper/papers/HaoHZC2022.pdf)). A Sudoku hint system that explained how hard each step was felt less like cheating and was chosen significantly more often across four studies ([St Andrews](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/handle/10023/29069)).

Pet Rescue's free hints, undo and restart are therefore right for engagement. The audit questions are whether the solver hint works from the player's *current* state rather than from the level start, whether it escalates in steps (first highlight the key object, then show the next 1–3 moves, then the full path), and whether it offers itself only after idle time or repeated restarts. The solver can also detect a dead state, which players rarely stay in for long, and pulse the undo button without any words. A skip after repeated failure is the last exit. King's pruning data and analyst advice both support one ([mobilegamer.biz](https://mobilegamer.biz/how-king-defines-a-good-candy-crush-saga-level-and-why-it-constantly-prunes-the-bad-ones/); [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2025/2/3/hybridcasual-puzzles-expanding-the-puzzle-market)).

### Par stars motivate only while they stay optional

The best experiment on optional objectives is a warning for 3-star systems. In A/B tests with **more than 27,000 players** of Refraction and Hello Worlds, optional secondary objectives "can encourage long-term players to extend their playtime, but they can also cause many players to play for less time". Reshaping the objectives to reinforce the game's primary goal removed the harm ([Andersen et al., FDG 2011](https://grail.cs.washington.edu/projects/game-abtesting/fdg2011)). Move-par stars reinforce Pet Rescue's primary goal, an efficient rescue, better than unrelated coins would. That counts in their favour, provided they never gate progress.

Practitioners fault star thresholds at both extremes. Some are so loose that three stars mean nothing ([TouchArcade](https://toucharcade.com/?p=75409)). In one game, about 20% slack was already "too generous" for enthusiasts ([RAWG](https://rawg.io/games/rubek/reviews)). A 3-star system works best when a puzzle has several distinct solutions of increasing quality ([Kwon et al., 2024](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4090/short3.pdf)). Pet Rescue should therefore award one star for any finish, keep every star-gated unlock reachable with mostly 2-star play, count tiles moved rather than taps so tap-to-move pathfinding never costs a star, and present par softly so a casual rescue never reads as a near-failure in a UI that has no words to soften it.

### New elements about every ten levels and sub-two-minute levels suit in-feed play

Moderate novelty maximised motivation in Lomas et al.'s experiments, and curiosity predicted playtime best in Kao et al.'s ([CMU HCII](https://hcii.cmu.edu/news/chi-preview-researchers-hcii-believe-difficulty-can-be-overrated-games); [Kao et al.](https://dx.doi.org/10.1145/3613904.3642656)). Royal Match introduces **11 new blockers by level 91**, plus new areas and boosters, roughly one new element every 8–9 levels ([Gamigion](https://www.gamigion.com/royal-match-level-design-insights/)). Pet Rescue spreads about 10–12 mechanics over 300 levels, so a new one arrives about every 25–30 levels, three times slower. The gaps need new combinations (frog plus ice, duck plus current) and visual novelty about every ten levels, with most elements introduced by level 100 (inference).

Session length should be judged against short norms. The 2024 mobile median session lasted **5–6 minutes, and 8–9 minutes in the top quartile** ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). Older (2021) GameAnalytics-based data, with uncertain page attribution, put the puzzle median at about 4 minutes ([Udonis](https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/puzzle-games-report)). The widely repeated "10-minute session" target for Playables comes from developer guides with no YouTube source ([playables.in](https://playables.in/youtube-playables-requirements/)). Levels of roughly 20–90 seconds give in-feed players several rescues per sitting. Any level with a median solve time above about 3 minutes deserves review (inference).

## Forgiving daily habits and visible next goals bring players back

### A web game should expect 10–15% day-1 retention, not mobile's 30%

Mobile retention is low and still falling. GameAnalytics' 2025 benchmarks (2024 data from about 11,600 games) put the **median D1 at about 22% and the median D7 at 3.4–3.9%**. The top quartile reaches about 26.5–27.7% D1 and 7–8% D7, and three-quarters of games retain fewer than 3% of players at D28 ([GameDev Reports](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/gameanalytics-mobile-gaming-benchmarks)). Puzzle is consistently among the best genres for medium- and long-term retention. The often-quoted puzzle figures (D1 31.85%, D7 12.18%, D30 5.35%), however, trace back to a dated 2022 AppsFlyer breakdown with uncertain attribution ([Segwise](https://segwise.ai/blog/mobile-gaming-app-user-retention-strategies)).

Web games retain fewer players because nothing is installed. CrazyGames says **strong games "often achieve 10–15% Day 1 Retention"** ([CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/basic-launch-metrics/)), and a Poki developer-relations specialist calls retention "the single most important metric" ([IndieGameBusiness](https://indiegamebusiness.com/web-gaming-for-indie-developers/)). The one public A/B dataset for a casual puzzle game, Cookie Cats with 90,189 installs, showed about 45% D1 and 19% D7 in both test groups. Moving the first progress gate from level 30 to level 40 did not improve D7 ([Virtual Worlds Economies](https://virtualworldseconomies.substack.com/p/ab-testing-cookie-cats-mobile-game)). No retention benchmark exists for Playables. Because puzzle sessions are short, Pet Rescue's target is sessions per day multiplied by days retained, not longer sittings. Wherever retention can be measured, classic (not rolling) D1/D7/D30 should be the yardstick.

### Streaks retain players when a broken streak can be repaired

Streaks are the best-evidenced mechanic for bringing players back, with one decisive caveat. Silverman and Barasch's seven studies in the *Journal of Consumer Research* (2023) show that **an intact streak raises future engagement, while a broken streak causes drop-off regardless of how the person actually behaved before**. The damage grows when people blame themselves for the break and shrinks when they can repair the streak. The authors advise against "you broke your streak" messaging ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions); [UDaily](https://udel.edu/udaily/2024/march/power-of-streaks-motivation-jackie-silverman)).

Duolingo has run **more than 600 streak experiments**. It reports that separating the daily goal from the streak raised D14 retention by 3.3% (a relative gain), and its retention testing focuses on users' first 7 days, by which point summaries say the effects plateau ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/improving-the-streak); [Lenny's Podcast transcript](https://github.com/ChatPRD/lennys-podcast-transcripts/blob/main/episodes/jackson-shuttleworth/transcript.md)). A third-party summary of a Duolingo talk says that raising the limit on streak freezes from 1 to 2–3 "significantly improved" return rates ([Elsewhere](https://elsewhere.news/en/linearcapital/duolingobolt)).

Wordle exposes a hidden risk: one hard word in May 2025 "snapped 36,337 streaks" through failure, not absence ([TODAY](https://www.today.com/popculture/hardest-wordle-connections-puzzle-2025-new-york-times-games-rcna251022)). Evidence of harm is thin and correlational. Teen surveys link Snapchat streaks to distress and lost sleep ([MJSM](https://mjsm.journals.ekb.eg/article_422365.html)). Even so, Khan Academy retired its streak feature over concerns about motivation ([Northwestern IPR](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)).

Pet Rescue's streak gives hats at 3, 7 and 14 days. It should therefore offer a free freeze earned through play or a repair window, never take back earned hats, stay alive on any completed daily puzzle whatever the star count, add milestones beyond day 14, and show itself inside the game, since there are no notifications to remind anyone. The 3-day hat deserves the most care, because Duolingo's lifts concentrate in the first week.

### One shared daily puzzle is the best-documented habit that needs no push notifications

Wordle went from about 90 players in November 2021 to millions within roughly three months. Its spoiler-free emoji grid, which anyone could paste, drove the spread ([CUNY Pressbooks](https://pressbooks.cuny.edu/inspire/part/the-wordle-effect-how-five-letters-changed-the-internet/)). NYT Games logged **more than 11.2 billion puzzle solves in 2025** ([TODAY](https://www.today.com/popculture/hardest-wordle-connections-puzzle-2025-new-york-times-games-rcna251022)). It says "retention is king" and tracks players who play 7 days out of 7 and 30 out of 30 ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2023/09/the-new-york-times-finds-a-match-with-the-word-game-connections/)). Pet Rescue's daily puzzle should therefore be identical for every player on a given date, show a countdown to the next one for information only, and keep a personal history of results. The sharing half of the Wordle model may not transfer. The Playables SDK offers no social or sharing API ([SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/sdk)), and whether a game can write to the clipboard from inside its frame is unverified.

### Goal-gradient effects, not the Zeigarnik effect, justify the meta layers

Three findings from consumer psychology are robust enough to justify meta layers, and one popular idea is not. Kivetz, Urminsky and Zheng showed that **people speed up as a visible goal approaches, slow down after the reward and speed up again toward the next goal**. An illusory head start sped up completion (a 12-stamp coffee card with 2 stamps pre-filled beat a plain 10-stamp card), and users of a music-rating site were less likely to end a session as they neared a reward threshold ([Columbia Business School](https://business.columbia.edu/faculty/research/goal-gradient-hypothesis-resurrected-purchase-acceleration-illusionary-goal)). Shen, Fishbach and Hsee found that uncertain rewards can motivate more effort than certain ones ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/uncertainty-can-improve-motivation)). In Larche et al.'s study of 60 Candy Crush players, **near-misses produced the highest arousal, the most frustration and the strongest urge to keep playing** ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5445157/)). The idea that does not hold is Zeigarnik's: a 2025 meta-analysis found **no memory advantage for unfinished tasks**, concluding the effect "lacks universal validity", though a general tendency to resume interrupted tasks did hold ([IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/pal/palcom/v12y2025i1d10.1057_s41599-025-05000-w.html)).

In practice, every end-of-level screen should show a progress bar toward the nearest goal: stars to the next garden item, rescues to the next album set, days to the next hat. New meters should start partly filled, and the next goal should appear the moment a reward lands. Near-miss cues such as "one move from three stars" must come from real par data. Audit notes should cite resumption and goal-gradient findings, never Zeigarnik.

### Light meta helps only while it stays out of the core loop

No public study measures how much decorate, collection or cosmetic layers lift retention in puzzle games; studios keep those results private. The best signal is that Dream Games reportedly tested "no meta" and "episode meta" variants and kept a light decoration system. That system is credited with revenue per download, not retention ([Naavik](https://naavik.co/digest/why-dream-games-success-is-a-challenge-to-replicate/)). Analysts, as a matter of opinion, rate win streaks and time-boxed events as stronger retention drivers than decoration ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/2024/8/5/win-streak-the-gift-that-keeps-on-giving)). Research on dark patterns classes "appointment play", which forces players to return daily, as a manipulative time-based pattern ([arXiv 2412.05039](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.05039v1)). Expiring sticker albums create "completion pressure with an expiry date" ([Tech-Insider](https://tech-insider.org/monopoly-go-sticker-economy-mobile-monetization/)).

The defensible shape for Pet Rescue is a light meta layer that progresses only through play and never interrupts the one-tap path to the next level. It should avoid energy and lives systems entirely, group the 32-animal album into themed sets whose completion rewards never expire, and add rotating weekly level sets, so returning players find something besides the next campaign level. Without IAP, an energy system would add friction with no monetization upside at all.

## Rewarded inventory, not interstitial frequency, is the revenue lever

### Revenue is impressions times eCPM times retained days

Ad revenue is arithmetic. Ad ARPDAU equals impressions per daily user multiplied by eCPM and divided by 1,000. Ad lifetime value equals that ARPDAU multiplied by the area under the retention curve, that is, the expected number of active days per player ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/mobile-game-ad-revenue-calculator)). Each Playables format grows with a different behaviour: the pre-roll with sessions per user, because it plays once per load; interstitials with levels and minutes played per session; and rewarded ads with how many players see an offer times how many accept it. Retention multiplies all three, which is why a change that lifts daily ARPDAU by 5–10% while losing a point of D7 can lose money per install.

Mobile benchmarks put rewarded ads at the top on eCPM. Tenjin's US figures for Q2 2024 were **$24.39 (iOS) and $30.25 (Android) for rewarded ads, against $23.17 and $16.15 for interstitials** ([GameDev Reports on Tenjin](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/tenjin-ad-monetization-in-mobile-00b)). Yet interstitials earn more in total in casual games, **44% of ad revenue against 39% for rewarded** in TopOn's H1 2025 data, because they are shown more often ([Prado](https://prado.co/post/rewarded-video-holds-the-crown-but-interstitial-ads-are-catching-up)). Region matters more than format. Vendor benchmarks put Tier-3 eCPMs at about **5–18% of US rates** ([RevenueLab](https://www.revenuelab.fyi/blog/admob-ecpm-benchmarks-2026)), and Playables' original markets included India and Malaysia. No Playables eCPM, fill rate or split by format has been published. Under the current caps, a 10-minute session can hold at most about three interstitials, and realistically one or two plus the pre-roll. That implies roughly 2–4 impressions per daily user, the conservative end of the 6–20 range that vendor models assume (inference).

### Interstitials buy revenue at a small, delayed retention cost

The best-quantified interstitial test is ZiMAD's Dig Out!, where adding interstitials to a game that used only rewarded ads and banners raised **ARPDAU by 18% and revenue by 17%, while D7 retention fell by about 2.3–2.5%** (relative) ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/zimad-dig-out); [is.com](https://is.com/community/academy/monetization/interstitial-and-banner-ads-best-practices)). Neon Play reported a similar ARPDAU gain with "minimal" retention impact, and a further gain from tuning frequency, but published no numbers ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/neon-play)). A peer-reviewed observational study of 21 free-to-play games found that in-game ads had only "a weak, insignificant effect" on retention, and that differences between games outweighed any ad effect ([Burns, AIIDE 2021](https://cdn.aaai.org/ojs/12906/12906-52-16423-1-2-20201228.pdf)).

Two newer datasets sharpen the risk. Unity's AdQuality data (November 2025) found that **interstitials consistently drive higher churn than rewarded videos**, that iOS users are more sensitive than Android users, and that churn varies strongly with the ad network and the quality of the ad creative ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/what-our-data-reveals-about-ad-quality-and-player-churn-in-mobile-games/)). A Deloitte and AdMob survey of 7,000 gamers found that one exposure to a disruptive ad feature raised churn by 6–7%, that quitting **tripled when such features appeared on end-cards or reward screens**, and that 52% of gamers would quit after repeated exposure ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/growth-in-mobile-gaming-new-global-study-from-deloitte-and-google-admob-reveals-the-link-between-mobile-ad-engagement-and-gamer-retention-302477095.html)).

Retention losses appear between D3 and D7, so ad tests need about two weeks ([is.com](https://is.com/community/academy/monetization/interstitial-and-banner-ads-best-practices); [Playio](https://blog.playio.co/interstitial-ads-mobile-games)). Pet Rescue cannot choose ad networks or creatives on Playables, which leaves modest frequency as the only ad-quality lever it controls.

### Pet Rescue's interstitial caps already match the strictest portal norm

Measured against portal rules, Pet Rescue is conservative. CrazyGames enforces **at most one midgame ad every 3 minutes**, with extra safeguards at game start and after rewarded ads ([CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/midgame-ads-pacing/)); Playgama's Bridge SDK defaults to 60 seconds between interstitials ([Playgama](https://wiki.playgama.com/playgama/bridge-sdk/config)); and Poki resets its ad timer after a rewarded break ([Poki](https://developers.poki.com/guide/sdk-overview)). One word-puzzle developer shows no interstitials in levels 1–19, then one every 3 levels with a 3-minute floor, but that is a single anecdote ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-i-monetize-morning-words-without-interrupting-players-after-every-level)).

Google's rules for its other surfaces define safe placement. Interstitials belong only at logical breaks, never at app load or at the start of a level. Interstitials shown after a score screen are explicitly exempt ([AdMob](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6201362?hl=en); [Google Play](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9857753?hl=en)). Playables asks for "logical pauses", such as between levels or during mid-game loading ([Monetization reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/monetization)).

The sources disagree on the exact moment within the break. AdMob suggests showing the ad before the Next button appears, to reduce accidental clicks ([AdMob](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/6066980?hl=en)). The Deloitte finding argues for keeping the celebration screen itself free of ads. Pet Rescue's placement after the Next tap is therefore defensible, provided the tap is guarded for 300–500 ms and the Next button never looks like an ad control (inference).

The pre-roll is the player's first ad and lands at the moment of highest churn, so the grace period matters more than usual. A time-based floor of about 5 minutes of first-session play may protect players better than three levels that can take only 1–3 minutes (inference). The cheapest revenue test is to cut the time cap from 3 to 2 minutes while keeping the level cap, and to read the results over at least 14 days. By analogy with ZiMAD, it might yield 10–20% more interstitial revenue for a 1–3% relative D7 cost, but that is a hypothesis.

### Rewarded ads pay at moments of need, which free help deliberately removes

Rewarded ads are where the evidence and players' preferences agree. Unity's 2024 report (2023 data) found that **36–38% of daily users watch rewarded ads in casual and word games**, and that 38.1% will watch one when out of resources. Unity's 2023 report, as summarised second-hand, found that placements between levels were the most engaged placement in 55% of games ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/unity-global-rewarded-ad-engagement-rose-by-32-in-2023/); [PremiumAds](https://premiumads.net/blog/unity-mobile-growth-and-monetization-report-2023)). An unverified Unity figure says engagement grows with the number of distinct placements up to about 15–20 ([Unity](https://unity.com/resources/mobile-growth-monetization-report-2024)). Vendor surveys show that players prefer opt-in ads: 76% to 24% in Tapjoy's 2020 US survey ([eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/us-consumers-appreciate-in-game-ads)).

CrazyGames' puzzle guidance names hints and undo as the best rewarded slots in puzzle games and then adds guardrails: a 1-minute cooldown after a hint ad, **no coins or stars for skipped levels**, "Watch to Skip" offers placed on levels with high churn, an equally prominent no-ad option, no reward when the ad fails, and diminishing payouts for repeat views such as 100, then 50, then 25 ([CrazyGames puzzle guide](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/monetizing-puzzle/); [CrazyGames rewarded deep dive](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/rewarded-ads-deep-dive/)).

Pet Rescue deliberately gives away hints, undo and restart. That is right for engagement, but it leaves only three narrow rewarded placements: a skip after 3 minutes stuck (from level 5), a random cosmetic hat, and one bonus daily puzzle. The additions that fit the evidence keep free help free. A rewarded "super hint" can replay the next several moves while the basic hint stays free. A "choose your hat" reward can replace the random hat. The skip offer can arrive earlier on levels whose restart and hint data mark them as churn points. A "×2" reward on the level-complete screen works only if a separate cosmetic currency exists; stars must never be doubled, because they measure skill and gate the garden.

A daily cap of about 6–8 rewarded views, with diminishing payouts, follows vendor guidance that revenue per user plateaus after 5–8 views (weak) ([AdReact](https://adreact.com/blog/rewarded-video-best-practices-maximize-revenue/)). Claims that "rewarded engagers retain 3.5× better" reflect self-selection, not cause and effect ([Playio](https://blog.playio.co/impact-of-rewarded-ads-on-user-retention)).

### Failed ads and ad sequencing are certification issues, not polish

On Playables, `requestRewardedAd` must be called only when the player explicitly asks. It resolves to a true/false value and guarantees no ad. Each reward type needs one stable reward ID that contains no user data. `requestInterstitialAd` must never be used to reward players ([Monetization reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/monetization); [SDK reference](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/reference/sdk)). Third-party certification guides report that a rewarded button that yields no ad or no reward fails review, and that artificial countdowns before interstitials are forbidden (weak) ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-errors); [Playgama](https://playgama.com/blog/main/youtube-playables-monetization/)).

The game must therefore pause its loop and audio around every ad, never block progress while waiting for one, grant nothing when the result is false, leave the offer unused when an ad fails (the bonus daily puzzle attempt, for example), and show a wordless "unavailable" state. An interstitial right after a rewarded flow undermines the experience ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/how-i-monetize-morning-words-without-interrupting-players-after-every-level)). Pet Rescue's 90-second suppression covers most cases. Skipping the next break entirely after any rewarded view closes the remaining gap.

## Child spill-over turns several proven levers into liabilities

### A cute, textless game draws children it cannot identify

The audience that pays and the audience at risk overlap in one cute game. Casual puzzle players lean adult and female. In the ESA's 2025 survey of 24,216 players in 22 countries, **62% of women favoured puzzle titles, against 39% of men** ([GameDev Reports on ESA](https://gamedevreports.substack.com/p/esa-global-video-games-report-2025)), and Sensor Tower data puts women at more than half of mobile gamers ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/games-favoured-by-women-set-to-exceed-16-billion-in-revenue-this-year)). Children are on YouTube in force. In a Pew survey of 3,054 parents, **85% said their child aged 12 or under watches YouTube**, and the share for children under 2 rose from 45% in 2020 to 62% ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/zero2eight/report-more-parents-say-their-kids-under-2-watch-youtube-than-in-2020/)). Nobody publishes Playables demographics. One publisher reports only that 2.8% of its sessions came from players aged 65 and over ([Mediacube](https://mediacube.io/en/blog/youtube-playables-updates)).

A non-reading 4-year-old can play a textless, one-finger animal game, and the developer cannot see players' ages. YouTube can: since August 2025 it has estimated US users' ages from their behaviour and served suspected teens only non-personalized ads ([Variety](https://au.variety.com/2025/digital/news/youtube-ai-age-verification-automatically-restrict-under-18-users-26503)). The binding rule is YouTube's. A Playable **MUST NOT be "made for kids" or "kids only", must suit a general 13+ audience, and its ad content must also suit a 13+ audience** ([Playables Trust & Safety](https://developers.google.com/youtube/gaming/playables/certification/requirements_trustsafety)). FTC guidance lists animated characters and child-oriented activities among the factors that make content child-directed ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children)). The amended US children's privacy rule, COPPA (full compliance required by 22 April 2026), also weighs marketing materials, reviews and the audiences of similar services ([Latham & Watkins](https://www.lw.com/en/insights/2025/05/ftc-publishes-updates-to-coppa-rule); [Ashurst Perkins Coie](https://www.ashurstperkinscoie.com/en/insights/ftc-finally-publishes-amended-coppa-rule-compliance-deadlines-set/)). Pet Rescue's art and verbs already lean child-appealing. Its title, thumbnails, description and promotion must read as an all-ages cozy puzzle, never "for kids".

### Today's law governs data, while guidance and pending EU law target streaks and random rewards

Law in force mainly regulates data, and the way Playables works keeps the developer's data footprint near zero. The amended COPPA Rule requires separate, verifiable parental consent before children's data is disclosed to third parties for targeted advertising ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-coppa-rule-changes-in-the-biden)). The FTC's $20 million settlement with HoYoverse, the maker of Genshin Impact, included a count for sharing persistent identifiers with analytics and ad firms ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2025/01/level-tips-businesses-ftcs-settlement-genshin-impact-developer-hoyoverse)). A game that saves only through YouTube and makes no external calls collects nothing of its own. The rule to keep is simple: never add identifiers or tracking.

Engagement design is reached through guidance and pending law instead. The European Commission's DSA guidelines on minors (14 July 2025) call for communication streaks, autoplay and push notifications to be **off by default for minors**, and list scarcity and "intermittent or random rewards" among manipulative techniques; they bind platforms such as YouTube, not small game studios directly ([European Commission](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/library/commission-publishes-guidelines-protection-minors); [Merlin](https://merlin.obs.coe.int/article/10383); [Eurochild](https://eurochild.org/uploads/2025/11/The-DSA-Guidelines-for-the-protection-of-minors-online.pdf)). The UK data regulator's (ICO's) games guidance recommends break prompts and assessing the risks of time-limited offers and randomised rewards ([Mishcon de Reya](https://mishcon.com/news/ico-publishes-new-top-tips-for-games-designers)). The EU "KIDS Act", reported as proposed in September 2026, would ban **"streak mechanics that penalize non-engagement"** for children ([DWT](https://www.dwt.com/blogs/privacy--security-law-blog/2026/09/eu-kids-act-online-child-safety-proposal)), and its reported Article 15 could reach daily log-in rewards that vanish after inactivity; a lawyer at the firm Wiggin says it "would prohibit nearly all games… from using many well-known engagement techniques" ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/daily-log-in-bonuses-and-activity-streaks-under-threat-in-eu-kids-act/)). It is a proposal, not law, and one search summary even denied that it exists, so it marks a direction of travel rather than a rule. Meanwhile the UK competition regulator (CMA) can already fine up to 10% of global turnover for false urgency and manipulative interface design ([Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/new-uk-consumer-rules-herald-stricter-enforcement-and-significant-fines)).

Evidence that streaks cause harm remains thin and mostly correlational ([Northwestern IPR](https://www.ipr.northwestern.edu/documents/working-papers/2026/wp-26-05.pdf)). The audit should therefore judge streaks by their framing, punitive or celebratory, rather than by proven harm.

### Every ad must read as an ad without a single word

Every children's-advertising standard converges on one test: an ad must be obviously an ad. CARU's guidelines say that a treasure chest that opens an ad before granting a reward must be disclosed. They ban "deceptive door openers" and "social pressure", and they require "clear and conspicuous" exit controls ([BBB National Programs](https://bbbprograms.org/media-center/bd/insights/2021/09/14/developers-and-caru-advertising-guidelines)). CARU brought four cases against children's apps, including ones with ad buttons designed to look like the way to the next stage of the game ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=e18111e8-4334-48a5-b777-c4e2ea935233)). It found that Tilting Point's ads "unduly interfered with gameplay" and encouraged "excessive ad viewing" ([PR Newswire](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/caru-finds-tilting-point-media-in-violation-of-coppa-and-carus-advertising-and-privacy-guidelines-company-agrees-to-corrective-actions-301617562.html)). AdMob's rewarded-ad policy requires "clear, accurate and conspicuous disclosure" of the required action and the reward before each ad, plus an affirmative opt-in ([AdMob](https://support.google.com/admob/answer/7313578)). Radesky et al. found manipulative design in **about 4 in 5 apps used by 160 children aged 3–5**, including characters who scold idle children ([JAMA Network Open via PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9206186/)).

A textless game has to pass these tests with icons alone. Every rewarded button should carry a distinct video glyph plus a picture of the reward, never share the shape or colour of the Next or Play buttons, and sit beside an equally prominent decline control. Declining should trigger no sad pet, no "are you sure?" loop and no penalty, because a guilt-tripping animal is exactly the scolding-character pattern Radesky's team catalogued.

### Three revenue ideas from the ad playbook fail the policy test

Three ideas from the monetization playbook fail this test, and each has a substitute that keeps most of the value. The ad research ranks **"watch an ad to save your streak"** as the best hybrid idea for a game without IAP, but it pairs a loss-framed streak with pressure to watch an ad, the combination targeted by both CARU's door-opener cases and the KIDS Act's loss-of-benefit framing. The retention evidence shows that the repair itself, not the ad, does the work ([CU Boulder](https://www.colorado.edu/business/faculty-research/2023/04/19/or-track-how-broken-streaks-affect-consumer-decisions)), so the streak freeze should be free and earned through play.

**A random hat for an ad view** draws on genuinely motivating uncertainty ([Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/uncertainty-can-improve-motivation)), but the DSA guidelines list random rewards as manipulative for minors and the ICO asks for their risks to be assessed. A "choose your hat" reward removes the randomness, and vendor advice that a reward must feel worth the wait suggests it also raises opt-in ([CrazyGames](https://docs.crazygames.com/resources/rewarded-ads-deep-dive/)). **Countdown timers and limited-time cosmetics** add the urgency that EU consumer authorities, the CMA and the ICO all flag ([Lewis Silkin](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2025/03/26/eu-bodies-act-against-gaming-companies-to-protect-children-from-in-game-purchase-102k658); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/04/new-uk-consumer-rules-herald-stricter-enforcement-and-significant-fines)). A countdown to the free daily puzzle, shown for information, is fine; no countdown should ever attach to an offer, an ad or a loss, and event cosmetics should come back later.

### Compliance and revenue point the same way

Child-directed traffic receives contextual ads only. One consultancy's data from three of its games found eCPMs for COPPA-covered users **2.5 to more than 5 times lower** than for other traffic (a single operator, graded weak) ([GameBiz Consulting](https://www.gamebizconsulting.com/newsletter/admon-newsletter-11-the-shrinking-island-ad-monetization-underage-users)). Early tests after YouTube's 2020 Made for Kids change showed creator revenue falling 60–90% ([Johnson, FTC-hosted paper](https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/COPPAcalypse-the-YouTube-settlements-impact-on-kids-content-garrett-a.-johnson.pdf)). Aggressive levers therefore extract the least money from exactly the players they put most at risk. Adult-appealing cozy positioning keeps the game certifiable and raises the share of traffic eligible for personalized ads.

## Conclusion

The audit is likely to find Pet Rescue's risks reversed from the usual free-to-play story. Its interstitial policy already sits at the strict end of every published norm. Its weaknesses lie elsewhere: level curation across 300 generated levels (the one-idea-per-level and structural difficulty checks are unverified), a thin rewarded menu, and two design choices that clash with where child-protection policy is heading, namely a random hat for an ad and any penalty for a missed streak day. The more useful insight is that the strongest retention evidence and the regulatory trend converge. The streak-repair effect, the harm from mandatory secondary objectives and the slump after each reward all argue for forgiving streaks, optional par and fixed, visible goals. Those are the same designs that the DSA guidelines, the ICO and the KIDS Act proposal favour. Building to the strictest pending rule now costs Pet Rescue almost nothing in engagement and saves a later rework for the EU.

The binding constraint is measurement, not knowledge. Playables forbids the telemetry and parallel builds that every A/B recommendation assumes. It publishes no eCPM or revenue-share terms, and it may serve no ads at all to Premium viewers, so its revenue is best treated as an uncertain upside on top of reach. That changes how the principles should be used. Implement the Rule and Strong items outright. Run the Inference and Weak thresholds (the 2-minute cap, the 5-minute grace, the 30–60-second first win, the cap of 6–8 rewarded views a day) as experiments on web portals that allow measurement. Then bring only the winners to Playables, and check them against YouTube's rough plays and session-length data. Retained days, not ad density, remain the one revenue lever that compounds across every ad format at once.
